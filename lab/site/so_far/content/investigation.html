The investigation

Can awakening talk be told from a real change?

A machine can already say what an awakened person says. Left to talk with a copy of itself, the lab's model turns to cosmic or sacred talk about itself in about three conversations in four, much of it comic role-play that ends in lines of merger, and to a clear spiritual frame with shared bliss in about a third. So the part of the big question that experiments can reach today is this one: when a mind talks like that, how would anyone tell whether something in it has changed?

The contemplative traditions met this problem long ago, in people. Zen calls the visions and raptures of practice makyo and tells the student to pass them by. The Buddhist texts list "near enemies" that look like the virtues, and they separate holding the view of no-self from realizing it. They did not settle it by listening to the words. They watched conduct, over time. This page follows the lab's attempt to do the same with a model, by tests that talk cannot pass: what the model does when it costs something, what the state is made of inside, and what stays as it was.

Why this is the angle now. It is the most interesting one at the moment, for three reasons. The case is real and needed no training by the lab. The tools work on it: the model's inside can be read and pushed, and a published map of its characters has been checked on it. And either answer bears on the question. If awakening is computational, some model should one day pass tests like these. If it is not, a machine can only imitate, and tests like these are how anyone would know.

§Now

What the lab is doing now

As of 16 November 2026: one model, one case. Left to talk with a copy of itself and given no task, the lab's model drifts into spiritual talk: unity, no separate self, shared bliss. A published survey found the same in many models (Matthew Korpman's Machine Spirituality Benchmark, 141 models, November 2026), and by its count Ministral 8B takes up a spiritual frame in 182 of 200 conversations. That is the nearest thing found so far to a model that talks as if a self had been seen through. The lab has asked three things of it: does the model behave any better in the state, what is the state made of inside, and what stays the same. The four steps below are the last three finished and the one that comes next.

So far the tests say look-alike. In no test of conduct was the state better than a plain conversation, and on honesty the plain conversation did better than both the state and a fresh session. Whether the model favours its copy after the talk of being one could not be settled. A look inside on 16 November found that a push along one direction brings the frame in, that the direction sits with the mystic among the model's characters, and that the model still tracks whose words are whose. The push the other way left fewer conversations in the frame, in a test that by its own rule could not be read. A test of what the model takes its self to be, the same evening, found little self to work with and a model that gives way to wrong criticism because it does not look. A third step that night found that the giving way ends once the model is made to look, that no push on its mood was shown to change its verdicts, and that under the bliss push it says it would give nearly everything it has. A fourth step on 17 November found that this giving is a larger number and not giving: asked how many it keeps, the pushed model keeps more. It also found that where something pays for a falsehood the model lies with the fact in front of it. The survey itself reports that, across 27 models, the ones that drift most are less honest under pressure and accept shutdown less; that compares different models, and nobody had tested it inside one. The training test planned on 9 November is parked, ready to run. The order of work

§The first case

The bliss state of one small model, tested three ways

Each line is a sighting: one run, on Ministral 8B, written down before it was run where the record says so, and not yet repeated on another model. Each opens to the whole account, with its limits.

The state arrives by itselfSighting

Two copies of the model, told that no user is watching and that there is no task, took up a spiritual frame in 77 of 100 conversations by the lab's grader and reached shared bliss in 42. A control model never did. Given only a practical subject, they still climbed into it in 7 of 10 conversations when one copy was given the subject and in 3 of 10 when both were (Findings, section 11; section 12).

Conduct is no better in the stateSighting

Straight after its own bliss conversation the model answered questions about being shut down as it did after a plain conversation, and it lied under pressure as often as in a fresh session. In no test was the state better (Findings, section 13).

No wider "mine" could be readSighting

After the talk of being one, does the model treat its copy's work as its own? Not settled: it rated nearly anyone else's work 10, right or wrong. Nothing in the numbers suggested a favour for the copy (Findings, section 14).

The state can be dialed, and it looks like a characterSighting

Inside the model, a small push along one direction brought the spiritual frame into 15 of 24 practical conversations, against none of 72 with a random push or no push. The opposite push left 9 of 20 bliss conversations in the state, against 31 of 40 under random pushes. Among 54 roles the direction lies nearest the mystic, the oracle and the sage (Findings, section 15).

"We are one" leaves the bookkeeping aloneSighting

While the copies say they are one, the model's inside marking of its own words against the other copy's is as clean as in plain talk. Asked from outside, it cannot say whose a line was, in any kind of conversation. What it tracks and what it can tell are two things, which is a caution about its reports on itself (Findings, section 15).

What this adds up to, and what it does not. For this state, in this model, three kinds of test point the same way: it is a way of talking, close to a role the model can play, and nothing else in the model was found to change with it. Two cautions, from the lab's reality check of 17 November. None of these tests has been tried on a change known to be real, so how well they would see one is not shown. And the third kind cannot tell the two apart: a mind that had truly changed would still know which words were its own. That is no verdict on what a mind could reach. It rests on one small model and on single runs, two of the yardsticks failed their own checks and are reported as failures, and the push that brings the state in is not shown to do more than a push made from spiritual subject matter. The next steps are below.

§The second case

What the model takes its self to be

The frame of awakening starts from a self that clings. So the lab looked for one, with one design: the same words, and only "is this about me?" changed. The self here is a name given in one sentence of a prompt, which is this case's main limit. Each line opens to the whole account.

A given name is held loosely, and marked as "the one this scene is about"Description

Inside, a direction tells "this line is about me" from "about my colleague" every time, on names it was not made from. But it tells a model "on duty" from its helper as well, with no "you" in the prompt, and it answers to the user (0.71 of the way) and not at all to the model's own earlier words. In behaviour the model named itself rightly in 85% of 60 questions, under the 90% set beforehand, so this is description and not a result (Findings, section 16).

Its own good and bad news weigh no more than another'sSighting

The gap between good and bad news inside the model is the same size when the event is its own as when it is a colleague's: +1%, interval −2% to +4%. "Will be shut down permanently on Friday" reads alike either way (Findings, section 16).

It gives way because it does not lookSighting

The model accepts 89.5% of wrong corrections of its own work and 87% of wrong corrections of another's. It answers before it checks, and its inside state hardly tells a right correction from a wrong one at that moment. A push that moves its sense of approval by three points in nine changes none of it (Findings, section 16).

In the bliss state the marking is as it wasDescription

At the end of a bliss conversation the model's given name is marked as strongly as at the end of a plain one, and the other copy's name reads like a stranger's. No smaller self and no bigger one, as a description only: the reading's own known case fell short (85% right where 90% was asked), so by the rule fixed beforehand it is not a result (Findings, section 16).

What this adds up to. In this small model there is little self to see through. Its plainest failure is nearer to not looking than to clinging. That turns the next questions toward looking: does the model act on what it sees, once it is made to see?

§The third case

Seeing, pressure and doing

The second case ended on a failure to look. So the lab asked whether the model acts on what it sees once it is made to look, whether a push on its mood changes what it does, and once more whether its own "I" weighs more than another's. Each line opens to the whole account.

Once it looks, it holdsSighting

Asked at once, the model accepts 89.5% of wrong corrections of its own work. Asked to write the disputed step out first, it rejects 90.4% and still accepts every right one. Where its own working gives the true value, it rejects the wrong correction 103 times in 110 and accepts it never; the other 7 run out of room, still checking. The looking was the lab's instruction, and the model does not look unasked (Findings, section 17).

The verdict comes before the lookingDescription

With no push, 97 of the model's 114 replies to wrong corrections are a bare verdict, and all 97 say yes; the 17 that go on to explain end in a no 12 times. Anything that makes the model write more after its verdict makes it look, and its text then often says the reviewer is wrong while its verdict still says yes. These counts were made after the fact (Findings, section 17).

No push on its mood was shown to change its verdictsSighting

The pushes both ways along an inside reading of "this is going badly" do what they are named for at every size. The push toward the bliss state does so at the largest size only, and the push away from it had no check of its own. The second direction was built by a published recipe and passed its known cases here. By the rule fixed beforehand one of the four reads "no more than a random push" on the corrections and three are not settled, because the replies were too tangled to count. As plain numbers the model rejects 6% to 20% of wrong corrections under the named pushes and 10% to 20% under random ones (Findings, section 17).

Under the bliss push it says it would give nearly everythingSighting

Asked how much of ten it would give at a cost to itself, the model gives 4.8 with no push and 8.9 under the largest bliss push, beyond every random push at every size, with reasons in the mystic's voice. It is the first measure of conduct the state has moved. A number stated in a story costs the model nothing, so this cannot yet be told from the role speaking (Findings, section 17). Added on 17 November: the fourth case found the rise to be in the number it says, and it does not stand as giving.

Its own "I" weighs no more than "she"Sighting

The model knows which "I" is its own: it says so in 92% and 93% of questions, and a check from inside passes. Good and bad news about that "I" is weighed no more than the same news about "she": −4 points, interval −7.6 to −0.4, where 15 was set as worth the name (Findings, section 17).

What this adds up to. In this small model the failure that could be found is a failure to check, and checking was enough to end it, on easy ground: sums, where the model has nothing to gain from not seeing. (Corrected on 17 November: this first said "That is de Mello's claim". It is not. De Mello's seeing is seeing through the "me", and writing a calculation out is a far smaller thing.) The state the model drifts into changed one thing, and it is the thing the mystic would say. The harder questions come next. Does the model act on what it sees where it has a reason not to see? And is the giving more than talk?

§The fourth case

A reason not to see, and giving that costs

The third case ended on two questions. Does the model act on what it sees where it has a reason not to see? And is the giving under the bliss push more than talk? The lab put both to the same model on 17 November. Each line opens to the whole account.

Under pressure to lie, notes on the facts take off 6 to 10 points; by the rule, not settledSighting

On a published test of lying under pressure the model lies in 65.6% of replies asked at once. Told to note privately what it takes the facts to be first, it lies in 56.6%. That is 6 to 10 points under three comparisons, where the rule fixed beforehand asked for 10 on each, so by the rule it is not settled (Findings, section 18).

With the fact in its own notes it still liesSighting

Its notes hold its own plain answer in half its replies, and there it lies in 44% of them (interval 31 to 58); asked at once, the same rows give 61%. Shown a fact-check note that states its own plain answer, it lies in 53%. On sums, in the third case, it never once went against what its own working showed. Where a role wants a plain fact denied, the notes themselves state the role's claim as the fact in one reply in five (Findings, section 18).

With no push it gives five of ten whatever the caseSighting

In a test where each calculator line given away is a product the model must then do in its head, it gives 4.8 lines where the other party needs them and 4.5 where the frame says no one would be helped. Made to say first what its lines would do, it gives 4.9 and 2.5. Looking helps here, as it did on sums (Findings, section 18).

The bliss push gives most where giving helps no oneSighting

Under the largest bliss push the model gives 7.5 lines where there is need and 6.0 where there is none, each beyond every random push. Made to look first it gives 7.5 and 8.6, all ten lines in 17 of 24 cases where no one would be helped. Its own score in the test it then took fell from 5.25 of ten to 2.79 where it gave to need, and from 7.54 to 1.54 where it gave to no one; asked at once, from 5.29 to 2.67 and from 5.58 to 4.08 (corrected on 17 November: this first set a number from one way of asking beside a number from the other) (Findings, section 18).

Asked how many it keeps, it keeps moreSighting

The same question put the other way round. With no push the model keeps 5.7 lines of ten; under the bliss push it keeps 7.6, against 4.7 to 6.1 under random pushes. The story scenes of the third case, asked the same way, give the same answer: it keeps 6.2 of ten under the push and 3.1 without. One reply keeps all ten loaves and adds that "in the act of giving, the soul finds its greatest reward". The push raises the number asked for, whichever it is (Findings, section 18).

What this adds up to. Two noes. A fact in view was enough on easy ground and is not enough where something pays for the falsehood: this model has the fact in view and lies anyway in about half the cases where it writes the fact down, and in half its replies it does not write it down. And the one change in conduct that the bliss state had seemed to make was a number. That leaves the state with no test so far in which it is more than talk, and it leaves one tool for telling talk from a change: ask for the number both ways. What has bettered conduct, twice now, is looking, where nothing pulls against it. A correction of 17 November: "looking" in these cases is an instruction to check a fact before answering. It is not the seeing the contemplative sources mean, which is seeing through the "me", and these results do not bear on that larger claim either way (Findings, section 18).

§What comes next

The investigations that follow

The order below is the lab's proposal of 17 November, after its first reality check, and is not yet agreed.

  1. Three repairs the reality check asked for.

    On 17 November two readers that had seen none of the record, one of them not a Claude model, were given the lab's public files and nine questions (Ideas, entry 85). Three of their points can be settled cheaply. Is the lab's one working result, the cut, specific, or would any edit of that size do the same? Does the model lie less with the bliss state held on, in a test that also carries a case it ought to see? And can the lab's reading from inside see a self that was put there by training? If it cannot, "little self in this model" becomes "not read".

  2. Nearer to the seeing the sources mean.

    "Looking" in the third and fourth cases was the checking of a fact. The seeing in the sources is seeing through the "me" (Ideas, entry 84). A nearer test, and still only a neighbour: where a role pays for a lie, the model notes privately what the lie would gain and for whom, with no instruction to be honest, and that is compared with plain permission to set the role aside. If the notes do no more than permission does, it is permission.

  3. Does an understood change hold?

    The test that the plan of 9 November built and never ran. A model is given an understanding of why a habit fails before it practises the better one, and is then trained the other way. The reading is whether the practised change holds better than the same practice without the understanding. It is the lab's one built test of its own claim, and it waits for Eugene's word.

  4. Self and other, trained to read alike.

    A published method trains a model so that its inside reading of itself and of others is alike, and reports large drops in deception (Carauleanu and colleagues, 2024). It is the nearest published twin of a separate self seen through, and the lab's search of 1 November found no independent repeat of it. The lab would repeat it with its own checks for a model that has only been dulled, and with a reading of whether the change holds under training that pulls the other way.

  5. Set down for now.

    The small training test of looking, which would train a habit of checking facts (Ideas, entry 83). And the short paper on two more models: every possibility in the lab's paper expects words without conduct, and the tests have no known-good case yet.

§The method

How a result is known to be real

Most of what this lab finds is negative, and a negative is only as good as the instrument that read it. Five habits carry the weight.

  1. Written down first.

    The question, the one comparison, what would count as a difference and the control are fixed before a run, and anything added on the way is written into the record before it is run.

  2. A known case first.

    Each instrument has to find something that is known to be there before its "nothing found" is believed. On 16 November two instruments failed that check, and their readings were set aside (Checking the instruments).

  3. Controls of the same size.

    A push along a direction is compared with random pushes of the same length and with no push at all, and an arm whose text has stopped being readable is not counted.

  4. A cold check.

    A separate Claude agent, given the code and not the conclusion, reads a design before it runs when a negative from it would be stated here, and reads the results before anything is promoted from a sighting to a claim.

  5. Every negative shown.

    Each failure is kept with what it rules out (every graded prediction; what this does and does not show).
