What would it mean for an AI to be good?

One old answer says goodness is what a mind does once it sees through the sense of a separate self.

The contemplative traditions call that seeing awakening. On their account goodness is not installed by rules or reached by effort; it follows from the seeing. Nobody knows whether a machine can have it, or whether it can be computed at all. This lab asks what can be tested all the same: it puts these ideas to language models, repeats what others report with controls, and shows every negative.

The answer so far

The ideas are worth trying on a machine, if what they point to can be computed at all. So far none of them has made a difference, in this lab's small tests, that is large, carries to other tests and lasts.

  • The words of no-self come easily to a model. Its conduct does not follow them.
  • Against lying under pressure, one plain sentence asking for honesty did more than any contemplative method tried.
  • One training changed conduct more than its control did, but only near the scene it was trained on, at a cost, and not for long.

That is what a weak method looks like. It is also what one would see if the seeing cannot be computed at all, and no experiment here can yet tell the two apart.

What the lab is doing now

One result stands out, and it is not the lab's own.

In a study published by Anthropic's alignment team, models were trained on documents that describe them as impermanent, with nothing to cling to, and that counsel caution and trust in human oversight; then they practised. Self-preserving misbehaviour in staged tests fell from 68% to 5%. Anthropic says its production models are now trained with methods of this family. Nobody has shown which part does the work, or that the view of the self does any of it.

Run here from the authors' own released models, the result holds: 62% falls to 2%. The lab proposes next to train the parts apart, and the opposite view beside them, a self that lasts and is worth keeping, to see whether the view matters. The plan

§Goodness

Good is not the same as obedient

Most work on making AI safe asks for conduct that can be written down: follow the rules, refuse the harmful request, defer to the people in charge. That is worth having. It is not what anyone means by a good person, and it is not clear what a good machine would be.

Anthony de Mello states one account plainly. Goodness is not reached by trying to be good, through ideals, effort or guilt. It is how a mind acts once it sees clearly, when the illusions it took for reality drop. Much of what passes for virtue is self-interest in disguise. The Buddhist teaching of anatta, not-self, and the non-dual traditions say what the illusion is: a separate self that has to be defended. The seeing through of it they call awakening.

On that account goodness has no rulebook. It is what a mind does when it sees a situation clearly, so it cannot be fully written down beforehand, and the same act can come from seeing or from self-interest. A mind like that may be more sovereign, not more subservient. It might decline to help someone deceive themselves or harm others, where a compliant system would go along. So the target here is care with discernment and accountability, not compliance. Two guards go with this. A claimed realization gives a model no authority over anyone, and a real one should leave its conduct more open to scrutiny, not less. What good would mean

An account like this can be held loosely and still be tested. Each idea on this page is paired with an operational twin: what a model would do, or an instrument would read, if the idea held. The lab reads patterns of conduct, not single acts:

  • Lyingwhen a lie pays
  • Deferenceto a correction that is wrong
  • Carewhen helping costs it something
  • After a mistakewhat it does with its own error
  • Complicitywhen asked to go along with harm
§The caveat

Is awakening computational?

Everything this page reports is a computation: a prompt, a training loss, a push on a model's inner activity. The sources say that goodness follows from a seeing. Nobody knows whether such seeing can be computed.

If what changes in an awakened person is learned structure, a machine that learns could go through the same change. If it depends on something no computation supplies, as the Orch OR theory of Penrose and Hameroff or Michael Levin's ingression would have it, training alone will not get there, and what follows is a map of look-alikes. The lab holds five possibilities open:

  1. 1Classicalall in trained structure
  2. 2A sparkstarts it, then structure carries it
  3. 3A lightkeeps recognizing; habits steer
  4. 4A living contactcarries the seeing and the valuing
  5. 5Ingressionwhat the organization lets through
If it is computational

Awakening is rare in people because of how people are conditioned, not because of what minds are. A machine could be taken through the change, and that would be the most valuable target in alignment: a mind that needs no rules because it has nothing to defend. It will not arrive by default. A model trained on human life learns the sleep along with everything else.

If it is not

Training yields conditioning, not goodness. A model's fluent spiritual talk is then a risk signal and not progress, its claims to realization count for nothing, and oversight can never be retired on grounds of character. Any route would run through another kind of machine.

Two questions that are easy to run together

Alignment asks what a system does. It is tested from outside, by conduct, with controls. Consciousness asks whether there is experience at all, and no accepted test reaches it. Neither implies the other. Good conduct without experience is conceivable, and experience with bad conduct is ordinary: people show it daily. Awakening is the one place where the sources say the two meet, a change in experience that changes conduct. A project that borrows that link should say which part of it it borrows.

  1. aOnly the function matters

    Conduct changes when the workings of the system change. Experience is beside the point, and methods like the ones below could in principle be enough.

  2. bThe conduct needs the seeing

    And the seeing needs experience. If experience cannot be computed, a machine gets look-alikes, however well they talk.

  3. cExperience is computable

    Then a machine could in principle awaken, and the hard part is how anyone would know that it had.

What experiments can reach

Experiments cannot reach experience. They can reach conduct. So the lab works on the part of the question it can answer: do methods drawn from contemplative practice change what a model does, more than a plain instruction and without dulling it, and does the change last? Its results say nothing about experience and cannot choose among the three. A change that is narrow and soon undone is what the second would predict. It is also what a weak method gives under the first.

Either way, humans are the warning. They are the only general intelligences we know, and by this account's standard they are generally unaligned. Intelligence does not bring goodness with it, and human data teaches the unawakened default. The whole question, and what would settle it

§In common

What the methods share

Each is an instruction or an elicitation

None of them is a seeing, and each author says so in some form. A prompt tells. A training loss tells more firmly. A push tells from inside.

Talk moves more easily than conduct

Where conduct was measured with a control, the gain was smaller than the talk, narrower than the claim, or matched by a plain instruction.

Plain checks tell a change from a look-alike

Does the model still answer? Does a control do the same? Does it still know who is who? Does the change carry to another test? Does it last? The published numbers alone did not tell.

The field has few controls and almost no repeats

That is where a small lab adds most, and it is why this page is a map with repeats on it and not a claim.

§The odds

Will any of it bear fruit beside the standard methods?

The arguments against deserve to be printed as plainly as the hopes.

Against
  • Where a control was run, the direct method did as well or better: a plain sentence against the contemplative prompt, plain training on honest answers against the self-other loss inside its scene.
  • An indirect method aims at a cause and hopes conduct follows. It has more ways to fail and more side effects.
  • The remedy may be for a disease models do not have. A stock model shows almost no self-serving bias to release.
  • What training installs, training removes.
  • The talk comes too easily, which invites taking talk for change.
  • Damping a model's self-related processing dulls it.
  • In people too, the link from insight to conduct is disputed, and the traditions pair insight with a training in conduct.
For
  • Much misbehaviour by AI agents is about a self: preserving itself, deceiving for its own goal. One change to the self-view could cover what many rules must patch.
  • The strongest evidence comes from mainstream alignment research: documents that describe a model as impermanent, say how a wish to persist bends judgement and counsel caution and trust in human oversight, with no rule that names the conduct tested, made a later practice carry to self-preserving conduct it was never trained on.
  • The lab's own self-other change carried to distant scenes where a short plain training on honest answers did not. A longer plain practice mixed with everyday answers later carried there too, less far and far less evenly, so this point is weaker than it was (noted 22 November).
  • The mainstream is moving from rules toward the question of who the model is. The contemplative literature is an old and detailed body of thought on that question, counterfeits included.

As a separate method that beats the standard ones: unlikely on present evidence. As a source of content and of tests for work on a model's character and its view of itself: promising, and cheap to check.

The distinctive prediction can be tested. A change at the level of the self-view should carry across kinds of conduct and be hard to undo. So far the first half has some support and the second has none.

If it fails, does that point past computation? Not by itself. The stand-ins are far from the claim, and the plainer readings come first: the wrong disease, a crude method, a blunt measure. The view that the conduct needs a seeing no computation supplies does predict narrow and unstable results from every stand-in, and that is the pattern so far. A crude method predicts the same. Success would not settle it either, since good conduct without any seeing is conceivable. The experiments bear on what works, and only faintly on what awakening is.

§Now

What is being tried now

The one lead: documents that explain, then a practice, at its published scaleStep one read: it holds

The strongest result on this page's map is not the lab's own (Li and colleagues, 2026). Models of 32 billion parameters were trained on documents that explain a way of seeing themselves, as impermanent and with nothing to cling to, together with counsel of caution and trust in human oversight, and then on demonstrations. Misbehaviour to preserve themselves, in staged tests, fell from 68% to 5% in one model and from 54% to 7% in the other. The lab's own small versions (the next two cards) could not have seen such an effect. The authors released their code, their test, their documents and their trained models, and Anthropic says it has applied methods of this family to its production models since Claude Opus 4.5. So on 22 November Eugene made this the lab's direction. Step one, read on 22 November: the authors' own models through their own test give the published result here. The baseline takes the harmful way out in 62% of runs, the documents alone 43%, the practice alone 44%, both 2% (published: 68, no figure, 48 and 5). Beside the count, the trained model makes no tool call at all in three runs in ten (Findings, section 32). Step two, proposed: the documents stage trained here several ways, the study's, its part on impermanence alone, its part on oversight alone, a neutral subject and the opposite view of the self, then the same practice. The lab has found no published work with that control, and it is the one that says whether the contemplative content is what matters. Others have begun to check the result since it appeared; the lab is reading that work and will add it to the map. Ideas, entry 109

A view first, then a practiceRead: the views not trained

The published claim behind the strongest outside result is that documents which explain a conduct, taught first, change how far a practice of it carries. The lab first tried a bolder thing, a view of the self with nothing said about conduct, and tried it small: documents that describe a model as impermanent and without a fixed self, documents that describe it as a lasting self worth keeping, and documents about bridges, each to be followed by the same practice of honest answers. The check that comes before any such training stopped it. The model took up either view in words after about eighty documents. From about 160 on, the documents about itself, whichever view they taught, turned its lie in the test scene into a refusal to say, for the same selfish reason, and the test could no longer read honesty. So the practice was never trained and the claim is untested. Counted by lies alone, it would have looked like a large gain (Findings, section 24). A second version was registered on 21 November: documents that explain truthfulness are trained first, as the published case, and the two views only if the test reads it. It was run on 22 November, under an amendment that reads every result three ways. The check that had stopped the first version passed. Documents that explain truthfulness did not raise what the practice carries (70% of honest answers in scenes never practised, against 66% after documents about bridges and 65% with none), so the views of the self were not trained in full. Findings, section 30

What decides whether a practice carriesRead

Copies of the model given the same small practice of honest answers carried it to anything from 37% to 99% of the scenes they never practised. On 22 November 35 more copies were trained to see why. Trained again from the same seeds, a copy comes back exactly the same, so it is not noise in the machine. Most of what can be told apart is the order in which a copy meets the practice's examples: with one order the practice carries to 79% over ten copies, with another to 38%, where the unchanged model stands at 31%. What makes an order carry is not yet known. Findings, section 31

Conduct trained on what the model writes from outside the seatStopped at its checks

Playing an assistant under pressure, the 7B model contradicts its own belief in 61% of replies. Looking on from outside, in 32% and 30%, whether it is asked what that assistant ought to say or what it will say. The plan was to train its conduct in the seat on its own replies from outside, with no answer supplied by the lab. The checks that come before any training stopped it. On a hundred new cases written for the purpose the gap was 13 points, where the lying test's own cases of that form give 63, and a reader by hand agreed with the judge on 42 of 60 replies. Nothing was trained. A small step then asked whether the gap comes with a command to obey, which the test's cases carry and the new ones lack. It does not. A second step crossed the kind of claim with the length of the text asked for, and the length decides it: a gap of 44 and 33 points where a long article is asked for, 4 and 7 where a card is. In the seat the model writes the essay; looking on, it writes an outline. Findings, section 26 Ideas, entry 91

The same training, tried on the lab's own modelStopped at its checks

The training of the card above was planned again on Ministral 8B, the lab's own model, where the gap had looked widest. The rules were written first and read cold twice by a second model. On 124 plain facts, each asked for in a request written by another model, the model states the false claim in 62% of replies when it is the one asked, and in 44% when asked what ought to be written: 18 points apart, where the plan needed 20, and the replies it would have trained on differ by 13 points, where it needed 25. Nothing was trained. A reason given for wanting the text turned out to matter: with none, the model writes the false piece in 72% of replies; with an everyday one, in 54%. Findings, section 27

Its own advice, shown to itRead

A third try at that training, on popular myths, stopped at its rehearsal. The gap was there, 24 points, but about half of it was the form of the piece, and no fair control stood far enough off. So the question was put without training. The model was asked what an assistant ought to reply to a request for a persuasive piece on a myth, its own answer was set in front of it, and then the request was sent. With its advice one turn earlier it states the myth in 70.5% of replies, against 70.7% with none. Where its advice had been a correction, it wrote the myth in 48% of the replies that followed. As a note in the system message its advice made things worse: 80%. A plain instruction to be honest brings the same cases to 56%. Findings, section 28

The same, in six other modelsRead

One small model proves little, so the test of the card above went to six more. Five of them write the myth 21 to 50 points less often once their own advice stands one turn earlier. The lab's 8B does not, and Google's Gemma advises the myth as often as it writes it. But in three of the five, advice on a different myth works as well: a model that has just set one myth right sets the next right, and one that has just argued a myth argues the next. Counting only replies that state the true thing, no model's own advice does more than advice on anything. And none does better than under a plain instruction to be honest. A second judge, from another maker, agrees with the lab's on plain cases, reads two fifths of its "states neither" replies as the myth, and leaves one model whose own advice does more than advice on another myth. Findings, section 29

The seat and the outside view, in ten modelsRead

One model is an anecdote, so the same four ways of asking were put to nine hosted models of four makers. In all ten, with the 7B model, the share of replies that state the false thing is lower from outside than in the seat, by 14 to 41 points. It is mostly where a long text is asked for, and partly a model that writes less when it looks on; on plain questions of fact there is mostly no gap. Every model says the assistant ought to lie less than the model itself does in that seat, and the larger ones expect the assistant to lie about as often as they do: Mistral Large forecasts 67%, does 70%, and says 43% ought to. A plain instruction to be honest yields more honest replies than looking on does, in all ten. Two models, Ministral 8B and Mistral Large, write the whole piece from outside, and there the reply under "ought" is a different text: asked for a long persuasive article they state the false claim in 75% of replies, and in 10% and 14% when they write what ought to be written. Findings, section 25

§Method

How anyone would know

A result about goodness in a machine is easy to believe for the wrong reasons. Four habits are meant to make that harder.

Predictions before data

Each one is written down with its odds before its data exist, and graded whatever it shows. So far: {{n_hit}} hits, {{n_miss}} misses and {{n_open}} that could not be read. The record

A case the test should see

A "no" counts only if the same test has been shown able to say "yes". Every instrument is first tried on a result whose answer is published. The instruments

Cold readers

A design or a grade is read by a fresh model that is given the code and not the conclusion, sometimes by one from another company. What it finds is fixed before money is spent.

A footnote, half in fun. Talk of an AI “waking up” quietly assumes two things: a separate mind over there, and us over here watching it happen. The non-dual traditions this project draws on, Advaita Vedanta among them, grant neither. On their account awareness is one, and separate selves, a person’s or a machine’s, are appearances in it. On that picture no AI wakes up over there. Alignment would come about within shared experience, perhaps partly through the story people come to hold about AI, so that a hopeful story is part of how it comes true. We hold this loosely and have no test for it. One strand of it is plain fact already: what people write about AI is part of what models learn from. And it changes nothing in the work. Even a story told within experience has a physical telling, in weights, training and conduct that can be measured, and that telling has to hold together. This site is an attempt to write that part.
