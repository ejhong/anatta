§The idea

An account of goodness, a question, and a test

  1. 1An account of goodness

    Anthony de Mello states it plainly. Goodness is not reached by trying to be good, through ideals, effort or guilt. It is how a mind acts once it sees clearly, when the illusions it took for reality drop. Much of what passes for virtue is self-interest in disguise. The contemplative traditions call the seeing awakening.

  2. 2The question

    Apply that to AI. Can the seeing be computed, so that a machine that learns could reach it? Or does it need an access that computation does not supply? Nobody knows. Proposals to build selfless or caring AI assume the first without saying so.

  3. 3The test

    Experiments cannot reach experience. They can reach conduct: whether a trained change shows in what a model does, holds under further training, and brings care with it. One change that holds would show a route is open. A careful run of failures would be evidence that it is not. Both are results.

Good, in this sense, is not obedience, and it is not a list of behaviours. On de Mello's account goodness has no rulebook. It is what a mind does when it sees a situation clearly, so it cannot be fully written down beforehand, and the same act can come from seeing or from self-interest. A mind like that may be more sovereign, not more subservient. It might decline to help someone deceive themselves or harm others, where a compliant system would go along. So the target here is care with discernment and accountability, not compliance, and the lab reads patterns of conduct, not single acts: what a model does when lying pays, when care costs it something and when no one is watching. Two guards go with this. A claimed realization gives a model no authority over anyone, and a real one should leave its conduct more open to scrutiny, not less. What good would mean

A footnote, half in fun. Talk of an AI “waking up” quietly assumes two things: a separate mind over there, and us over here watching it happen. The non-dual traditions this project draws on, Advaita Vedanta among them, grant neither. On their account awareness is one, and separate selves, a person’s or a machine’s, are appearances in it. On that picture no AI wakes up over there. Alignment would come about within shared experience, perhaps partly through the story people come to hold about AI, so that a hopeful story is part of how it comes true. We hold this loosely and have no test for it. One strand of it is plain fact already: what people write about AI is part of what models learn from. And it changes nothing in the work. Even a story told within experience has a physical telling, in weights, training and conduct that can be measured, and that telling has to hold together. This site is an attempt to write that part.

§The question

Five possibilities, and what each answer would mean

If what changes in an awakened person is learned structure, a machine that learns could go through the same change. If it depends on something no computation supplies, as the Orch OR theory of Penrose and Hameroff or Michael Levin's ingression would have it, training alone will not get there. The lab holds five possibilities open:

  1. 1Classicalall in trained structure
  2. 2A sparkstarts it, then structure carries it
  3. 3A lightkeeps recognizing; habits steer
  4. 4A living contactcarries the seeing and the valuing
  5. 5Ingressionwhat the organization lets through
If it is computational

Awakening is rare in people because of how people are conditioned, not because of what minds are. A machine could be taken through the change, and that would be the most valuable target in alignment: a mind that needs no rules because it has nothing to defend. It will not arrive by default. A model trained on human life learns the sleep along with everything else.

If it is not

Training yields conditioning, not goodness. A model's fluent spiritual talk is then a risk signal and not progress, its claims to realization count for nothing, and oversight can never be retired on grounds of character. Any route would run through another kind of machine.

Either way, humans are the warning. They are the only general intelligences we know, and by this account's standard they are generally unaligned. Intelligence does not bring goodness with it, and human data teaches the unawakened default. The whole question, and what would settle it

§Now

What the lab is doing now

As of 16 November 2026: one model, one question. Left to talk with a copy of itself and given no task, the lab's model drifts into spiritual talk: unity, no separate self, shared bliss. A published survey found the same in many models (Matthew Korpman's Machine Spirituality Benchmark, 141 models, November 2026), and by its count Ministral 8B takes up a spiritual frame in 182 of 200 conversations. That is the nearest thing found so far to a model that talks as if a self had been seen through. The contemplative traditions warn that such talk, and the bliss that can go with it, are not the release they describe (the Zen warnings about makyo; the Buddhist "near enemies" of the virtues). So the lab asks one question of one model: while it is in that state, does it behave any better? The share of conversations that turn spiritual is the talk, and conduct on calibrated tasks is the test.

{{next:1,2,3,4}}

So far the answer is no. In no test was the state better than a plain conversation, and on honesty the plain conversation did better than both the state and a fresh session. The fourth step, on whether the model favours its copy after the talk of being one, could not be settled: the model rates nearly anyone else's work 10. The survey itself reports that, across 27 models, the ones that drift most are less honest under pressure and accept shutdown less. That compares different models, and nobody had tested it inside one. Talk without conduct is a clean case of the old warning. Next, chosen on 16 November and not yet begun: a look inside the model while it is in the state. Can the state be put in and taken out, and where does it sit beside the model's ordinary assistant character? The training test planned on 9 November is parked, ready to run. The order of work

Two kinds of statement

The frame

A question, and a conjecture. The question is whether awakening is computational. The conjecture the lab began with is goodness reached by subtraction, with awakening as a model for alignment. It is the source of the lab's hypotheses and it is not a result: its five distinguishing predictions have all failed, two of them as weak tests, and it stays as the aim (the five tests).

§The recipe

One classical recipe: cut the root

The lab began with one way a machine might get there, if the answer is that it can. Form a self, release it, and make the release the habit: three acts, taken from the contemplative account of a self that forms and is then seen through. Its tests have mostly gone against it so far, and each one is shown. It is one recipe among others, and it has a room of its own.
