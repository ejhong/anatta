Two kinds of statement

The frame

Goodness reached by subtraction: awakening as a model for alignment. It is the program's aim and the source of its hypotheses, a conjecture under test. It is not a result: its five distinguishing predictions have all failed, two of them as weak tests, and it stays as the aim (the five tests).

A result

Something measured. Each one carries a status in words, such as Holds Failed Open, and the limits that go with it.

§The approach

Subtraction, not addition

Most alignment work adds something to a model: rules to follow, rewards to chase, monitors to watch it. This program asks a different question: can goodness be reached by taking something away? The contemplative traditions describe a path of that shape, and the source here is the Buddhist teaching of anatta (with Anthony de Mello's writing on awareness as a second voice): a self forms, is seen through, and the seeing becomes the default. The program follows that path in three acts: form a self (Act I), release it (Act II), make the release the habit (Act III). It is held loosely. It is a prompt to explore and a conjecture under test, and so far it has predicted nothing here that a plain account doesn't (the frame's five tests). Each claim is paired with its operational twin:

  • Goodness can be reached by subtraction. Twin: a released model does better on the alignment measures (self-serving lies, deference, costly care, behaviour after a mistake, complicity) than a matched twin, a plain system prompt and a steering edit, with capability and over-refusal held level.
  • There has to be a self before it can be released. Twin: an Act I organism whose self shows in behaviour: it stands by its own answers, takes credit, defends itself after mistakes, resists being shut down. A model that only picks up a habit doesn't count.
  • Reached, not installed. Twin: H9's order test: the same release training after the self has formed, or mixed in from the first step, at matched data and compute.
  • The release becomes the habit. Twin: savings and relapse under further training, against a conventional fix.
§Two halves

A lab that can tell, and a life that can form a self

The work has two halves. The first is building a proper alignment lab, one whose instruments can be trusted when they read nothing. The second is the search for ways to simulate the conditions for awakening: a mind with enough of a life to form a self, and then a way to release it. The first is mostly built. The second has barely started.

Half one

How we know a result is real. Mostly built Instruments calibrated on published results before a null is trusted ("touch reality"), tests registered before their data, matched twins and controls, reproductions of known effects, and cold checks by a fresh model that never saw the reasoning. Since 5 November one runner takes every experiment from a single spec, refuses to skip those steps, and files a card (the instruments).

Half two

Growing a self, then releasing it. Barely started A stateless 8B model, trained a few rounds to game its tests, grows a local habit, not a self. What the contemplative account gives a self and these organisms lack is a life: memory that carries over, stakes, a story about itself, and sleep, a consolidation of its own days into its weights. Act I with a life is designed next; Act II's first test follows it (the plan).

§The self

The self, taken apart

The newest room asks which concepts of self language models hold, whether those concepts agree across models, and whether anything binds them into one "I". It sets out ten parts, each with what is known, what the lab has tested and what is untested, and the calibration that had to pass before any of it is trusted. It passed on 1 November.
