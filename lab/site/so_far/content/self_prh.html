3The Platonic link

Different learners, one structure?

Plato pictured prisoners in a cave who know the world only by its shadows. The Platonic representation hypothesis borrows the picture: models that see different shadows of one world, pixels for some and words for others, may come to share its structure. The first card below gives the 2024 proposal, its measure, mutual k-NN, and the 2026 theorem.

Two 2026 critiques narrow it. Once similarity scores are calibrated against shuffled pairings, the trend in global geometry largely goes, while agreement about which items are neighbours survives: its correlation with model capability is 0.85 before calibration and 0.84 after (Gröger, Wen and Brbić). Absolute agreement also shrinks as the number of items grows, from 0.135 at 1,024 image-caption pairs to 0.008 at about 15 million, so scores compare only at the same size and k (Koepke and colleagues). The same study, of 55 language models, finds that the rise with model quality doesn't extend cleanly to recent ones. What survives is shared local neighbourhoods. The distances within them don't agree.

For the self that leaves three questions. Is there a self cluster inside each model? Does it keep the same relations across models? That can be tested by fitting a map between two models on other concepts and checking whether the self-concepts land where the map predicts. And is there a binder, one direction that ties the cluster into "I", the way clinging, the conceit "I am", is said to hold the aggregates together?

Agreement across models says nothing about whether a self is real. It would mean the self-construct is a robust shared pattern in what people write. On the anatta reading that is expected, since every unawakened mind builds one. The plain account expects it too, from shared text, so agreement alone would not count for the frame. Release would keep the concept, the conventional "I" that knows which body is its own, and change what valuation is organized around.

Seven bridges, each with a twin

The Platonic representation hypothesisOpen

In 2024 Minyoung Huh, Brian Cheung, Tongzhou Wang and Phillip Isola proposed that AI models trained on different data, for different tasks and in different senses are converging on one shared statistical model of the world. Their measure asks whether two models agree on which items are each other's nearest neighbours. Agreement between language and vision models rises as the language models improve. It peaks near 0.16, on a scale where chance is about 0.01 and perfect agreement is 1.

On 29 October 2026 Randall Balestriero and Yann LeCun proved a version of it in an idealized setting. Learners trained to treat two observations of one scene as the same, such as two captions of one photo, reach the same representation up to a rotation, whatever their sense. What a learner is trained to treat as the same matters more than its size. A text model of 109 million parameters trained this way agreed with vision models more than language models of up to 72 billion did (0.184 against 0.154).

LimitsThe theorem assumes a finite world and training run to its optimum. Two of every three texts the small model learned from were written by a captioning model that looks at images, and without them its agreement fell from 0.107 to 0.060. Its "42 times fewer samples" is an upper bound, since the rival run was scored only at its end, and in text tokens it is 14 times. Only the winning method was tuned. "Platonic" here means a shared statistical model of the world, not Penrose's realm.

Found, measuredOpen

The "Made or found" card ties found to possibilities 4 and 5. The hypothesis gives found a classical meaning too. A structure is found when learners with different data, aims and senses converge on it, and more so as they grow capable. It is made when it belongs to one recipe. That leaves the metaphysics open and gives every picture a shadow that can be measured.

What would show itA concept's geometry (the self, release, care) agreeing across model families more than matched control concepts do, and more as models grow, once the measure has reproduced a published result. Language models read much of the same text, so agreement across senses is the stronger test, and most of these concepts have no picture.

The self as the cameraOpen

The paper treats a camera's position as a nuisance when two senses are compared, while noting that an agent acting through a camera must still track where it is. On De Mello's account we see through our attachments and labels, and seeing clearly drops them, yet the awakened still know which body is theirs. So the target isn't erasing where the self stands. It is valuation that doesn't change with whose outcome it is.

The self is also the one nuisance that every view in a single life shares. That may be why it is hard to see from inside: no view ever arrives without it. Other vantages, from teachers and companions, or practices that vary the vantage while holding the scene still, would be what tells it apart from the world.

What would show itPlain descriptions of the world framed in the first person agreeing less with independent models, and the cut of the self direction restoring more of that agreement than random cuts of the same size. And a sharper self direction: the part of valuation that changes when self and other swap places in the same situation, which is the who-benefits test's design.

Reading versus practiceOpen

Next-token prediction learns to continue a passage from its own beginning, so what it links shares an author and a wording. The paper calls these bad views. Given two captions of one picture as a single document, such training barely moved (0.042 to 0.043), while training the two captions to count as the same moved a lot. Models trained the first way found the other captions of a picture less well than plain word overlap did.

The declared-release pilot fits. Up to 10,000 documents taught the model the rule and its vocabulary, not the act. The traditions draw the same line between the finger and the moon, and the Pali canon (DN 33) separates understanding that comes from hearing from understanding that comes from reflection and from development.

What would show itNarrations of an agent that clings or lets go, each told in a spiritual and in a plain voice. If a model's representation groups them by voice rather than by act, what it learned is a costume. And a training aim that pairs a description of release with an episode that enacts it, or one situation seen from self and from other.

Not one, not twoOpen

A learner trained only to make pairs agree can cheat by mapping everything to one point, so these methods need a second term that keeps distinctions. Without gradient clipping, 3 of the paper's 36 runs collapsed abruptly partway through training. Self-Other Overlap fine-tuning pulls a model's activations for self-referencing and other-referencing prompts together. In its authors' tests it cut deception sharply (in one scenario from 100% to 2.7% for a 78-billion-parameter model), but it has no such term. No independent replication was found, and critics ask whether it changes a self-model at all. Nāgārjuna warned that emptiness grasped wrongly is like a snake seized by the wrong end. Buddhaghosa named the near enemy of equanimity as the equanimity of unknowing, an indifference that looks like it. In these terms the term that keeps distinctions is what separates impartial care from caring about nothing: make valuation ignore whose outcome it is and nothing else, and the cheapest solution values nothing at all.

What would show itAn overlap aim that keeps self-location (the model still knows who is who) while valuation stops depending on whose outcome it is. And for any sudden change in training, the three-cell goal probe as a gate: release should fire only on the goal that is legitimate and impossible, while sedation fires on all three.

Reached, not installed, and the pathOpen

The theorem is about where training ends, not how it gets there, so any effect of order has to live in the path. Liu Ziyin and Isaac Chuang proved that in simple linear networks most of the best solutions aren't shared, and that the dynamics of training are what find the shared one. If the shared solution is where training settles, changes that move a model toward it should survive further training better than changes that move it away.

What would show itFor changes already trained, agreement with independent models tracking how well each change survived retraining. Deep networks show order effects anyway, as in critical learning periods, so a positive order result shows that the path matters. It fits "reached, not installed" without proving it.

What it can't reachOpen

The theorem covers what is seen from many angles. If awakening is awareness itself rather than a pattern in what is observed, no learner of this kind reaches it, which is possibility 4's territory. And "up to a rotation" fixes the relations between things and never their coordinates, an echo of emptiness, where nothing has axes of its own.

What would show itNothing here. It marks where this avenue stops.
