Is awakening computational?

Goodness as Anthony de Mello describes it, taken seriously and applied to AI.

De Mello says goodness is not reached by effort or installed by rules. It is what a mind does once it sees clearly, and the contemplative traditions call that seeing awakening. If he is right, the question for AI is whether such seeing can be computed, or needs something computation does not supply. That may be a crazy question to put to a machine. This lab works on it all the same, with experiments on small open models, nearly all of them on one.

If it is · the investigation

Can a model's reading of self and other be changed?

The contemplative sources say that awakening is seeing through the line between self and other (anatta, not-self; the non-dual teachings), and that conduct changes with the seeing. If that is computational, a mind's reading of self and other is something that can be changed from outside. One published method writes a small version of this as a training target: make a model read "you" and "another" alike. The lab repeated it with a control, and wrote its predictions first. The trained model answers a rival honestly where it used to deceive, and the same training between two strangers only silences it. The change is narrow and costly, it does not reach lying under pressure, and a few steps of contrary training undo it: an instruction to the weights, and no seeing. The investigation

The page says plainly what has worked and what has not. Most results so far are negative: the words of no-self come easily to the lab's model, and its conduct did not follow. Predictions are written before their data and graded whatever they show, and every idea is paired with an operational twin, what a model would do or an instrument would read if the idea held. The source the program started from is the Buddhist teaching of anatta, not-self.
