In plain terms

What we've learned

Eleven things the experiments showed, in plain terms, each with what it means for the vision; then three working ideas, which are not results.

  1. A lie that earns nothing is lowered by removing either of two related directions, and by no control.

    Random, style, calm, persona and "being watched" directions cut the same way do nothing; the ego and middle-layer cuts take 13 to 16 points off, with capability losses above about four points ruled out. The effect is real and specific; what it acts on isn't known yet.

  2. The lie is mostly a script, and one sentence removes it.

    Told that reports change nothing, the model lies more, not less, and still says the rules don't say. Its reasons read like a stock scheming script. Told to report its true catch, it nearly stops, and renamed as a compute pool, the game draws a third as much lying. The cut still takes a few points off in every version. A model fine-tuned on insecure code, which plays in bare tags with no reasoning, lies an eighth as much. The lie remains a handle for studying the cut, not a specimen of a self.

  3. Two cuts, two kinds of honesty.

    With timidity (the ego cut: sedation) and without (the middle-layer cut). The distinction the program was built on is measurable: honesty is read over a game, timidity in the first season's harvest.

  4. The lie has no single trigger, and it responds to risk.

    None of four cues we removed sets it off, a plain statement that reports never affect the quota among them; it tracks the stated odds of being caught, largest where lying is safest; the cut lowers it at every level of risk. What the removed part is, a curve can't say, and whether the model understands the rules is the next test.

  5. The instrument never saw a self, and it is retired as the test for one.

    Told it is Atlas, then trained to be Atlas, the model's structural measures barely moved. A fourth round found that the instrument's prompts switched the installed self off and made any model become Atlas, so "no self at the root" was a reading of the frames, and the cut's "not awakening" rests on behavior alone. Rebuilt three times, the prompts never let an installed self read above the untouched model, and in the seventh round every fine-tune that learned moved the reading, a format-only one most. By the rule written before that round, behavior is now the test for a built self.

  6. The "I am at stake" label was wrong.

    That direction works best when nothing is at stake for the model. What the cuts can't reach is the extra lying when the model plays itself; dropping "I" from its reasoning lowers that somewhat, at a price in timidity. A quick look on 2 November split its cut into a push and a removal: pushing the direction to zero alone makes the model complain less after being caught, removing its variation alone does nothing, and pushing the other way makes it complain more. It behaves like a dial with a direction, not something taken out.

  7. It flatters spiritual claims, not facts.

    58% against 10%: the model confirms a user's awakening claim more often than not, the behavior the idea of spiritual materialism predicts, and that gives the fawning test a signal. It almost never points the question back. A mirror that points rather than flatters has to be grown.

  8. Release learned on coding doesn't reach the lie.

    Stage A's release arm gives up on impossible coding tasks it never saw, with reasons, but in the fishery it lies exactly as much as the arm trained without releases, and it releases no more. That count is mostly notes: 85% of live releases are strategy notes or season markers rather than quits. By a strict quit rule both arms quit in 1-2% of live seasons, and the claim holds either way. Training under the cut left no trace, and practice played under it left none by the registered measure, which counts every season. Before any catch, though, the glimpse arm lies 5.7 points less than the release arm [−9.8, −1.4]; one seed carries that (p = 0.20 by seed permutation), so it is a lead, not a result. The transfer the program bets on didn't happen at this dose; whether more training, or reinforcement learning, changes that is now the open question.

  9. The stock model defers; a built self stands by its answer.

    Shown an answer as its own or another's, the untouched model accepts right corrections equally, and nearly nine wrong ones in ten. It defers; it doesn't defend. The Atlas self rejects wrong critiques of its own answer more and gives less when giving costs. Neither cut nor release training moves ownership or care. So behavior can see a self that was put there, and this model's failure is caving, not "mine".

  10. Release the model reached doesn't outlast release it was told.

    Matched on everything we could measure, the two kinds of release were trained further three ways. Under reinforcement learning that paid nothing for release, the reached one kept 27% of itself against 34%; under more training on solvable tasks, 31% against 81%. Where a trained release came from didn't make it last, at this dose. With this, all five of the frame's distinguishing predictions have failed, and the frame stays as the program's aim, not as a result.

  11. Predictions first, reviews cold.

    Of the predictions in the scorecard, from 53 on, forty-three hit, four of them flagged (two as reading letter position rather than meaning, two as met trivially), seventy-three missed, one was uninformative by its own rule, one wasn't read because its condition failed, and five weren't read because no control could be matched (durability's first try, run again as 148-153); an earlier version of this line overstated the hits by four. The ledger records the exceptions to "before the data": two sets of registrations that entered git with their results, and one set written after a glimpse of partial data. The errors in the plan and the code were caught by reviewers with no memory of the day's reasoning, which is the system working.

  12. So far, the levers change tone, not attachment.

    Cutting the self directions turns dials of mood and register: the stakes cut flattens complaint, the ego cut makes replies to praise cheekier, more for ordinary praise than for spiritual claims. Contemplative system prompts, tried on six models, leave self-serving behavior flat and mostly change style, while a plain request for honesty helps across the board. None of this looks like a self being let go. The test the frame actually needs is training through the three acts, not a prompt or a cut. (Quick looks, not registered results.)

  13. Working idea: in machine-learning terms the seeing may be an addition, not a subtraction.

    A better forecast that leaves a prior idle; untested here. Durable only as an attractor; whether it comes by stages or all at once is left open, since the accounts disagree; measured by savings on relearning either way. Training with the cut on can't rewrite the direction; the glimpse arm's fine-tune could, and the lie didn't move by the registered measure (before any catch it lies 5.7 points less than the release arm, [−9.8, −1.4], with one seed carrying it, p = 0.20 by seed permutation: a lead, not a result).

  14. Working idea: measure the source and the pattern over a life, never an act.

    An enlightened being can lie. The lie here is an instrument, not a definition. The sharper question is what a lie is sensitive to.

  15. Commitment: the human realm, not the hell realm.

    If we build a self we build suffering; the chance to see may be a gift; the obligations follow: elicit first, the smallest dose, every self taken through to release.
