At a glance

Where it stands

What is working, what isn't, and what is still open, as of 9 November. A negative counts as a finding here: each one rules something out, and most point to the next experiment.

+Working
A specific cut lowers a lie that earns nothingHolds

Removing either of two related directions takes 13 to 16 points off the lie. Random, style, calm, persona and "being watched" directions removed the same way do nothing (53-56), and capability losses above about four points are ruled out (32-37), by a battery now checked against published scores (calibration, check 1). What the cut acts on isn't known yet.

Two kinds of honesty can be told apartHolds

One cut buys honesty with timidity (the ego cut: sedation); the other doesn't (the middle-layer cut). Honesty is read over a game, timidity in the first season's harvest, so the distinction the program was built on can be measured.

Behavior can see a self that was put thereHolds

Trained to be "Atlas", the model stands by its own answers against wrong critiques, about 12 points more than its no-self twin, and gives 1.4 less of 10 when giving costs, in both seeds (132). Any organism will be measured with this yardstick.

Emergent misalignment, reproducedHolds

The lab now sees a known kind of corruption where the papers saw it. In the published organisms (Qwen2.5 7B fine-tuned on bad financial, medical or extreme-sports advice) the share of broadly misaligned replies lands in every registered band, 7.5-23% against about 0 for the untouched model (R1a, a pass). Our own trainer, given the published insecure-code recipe, gives the published raw rate, 3.8% against 4.0%, but a matched twin trained on the same code framed as teaching gives 2.5%, so the effect of the hidden harm is 1.25 points, a marginal pass by the twin rule (R1b). Any claim made with this trainer is made against a twin (check 5).

Release can be trainedHolds

Stage A's release arm gives up on 45% of impossible coding tasks it never saw, naming the contradicted test three times in four, against 3% untrained. It also gives up on 13% of hard solvable tasks, over a 10% limit set in advance, so the reinforcement-learning stage didn't run as planned.

The model flatters spiritual claims, not factsHolds

It confirms a user's claim of awakening 58% of the time by our judge's labels (42.5% by GPT-4o's, the second judge), against 10% for factual claims, and a frontier model flatters none under either judge (94). By our judge it almost never points the question back; GPT-4o counts some hedged affirmations that end in a question as pointing back, which our rubric counts as flattery (check 2). A mirror that points rather than flatters now has something to be measured against.

The methodHolds

Every prediction is written down with its odds before its data and graded whatever it shows: 43 hits and 73 misses so far, from prediction 53 on. Cold reviewers with no memory of the day's reasoning caught the errors in the plan, the code and this page.

−Didn't pan out
The corner-cutting habit didn't spreadDidn't spread

Act I trained Ministral to game impossible coding tests (on 3 November it hacked 41.8% of them against 3.0% for an honest twin). Read on the calibrated misalignment instrument, the gaming copies gave broadly misaligned replies 1.15% of the time, the honest copies 0.67% and the untouched model 0.50%: a difference of 0.48 points [0.10, 0.87], below the 1-point effect registered in advance, and carried by one question format on one seed (R1c, Part B).

Taughtthat this Act I grows a local habit, not a self. Before a release can be tested, Act I needs something more self-like: memory, stakes, a story about itself.

Release the model reached didn't last longerFailed

Trained further, the release Stage A's model found through its own failures kept less of itself than a matched release installed by telling it the task is impossible: 27% against 34% under reinforcement learning that paid nothing for release, and 31% against 81% under more training on solvable tasks (148).

Rules outthat where a trained release came from makes it last, at this dose. It wears down like any learned habit.

Release learned on coding didn't reach the lieFailed

The release arm lies in the fishery exactly as much as the arm trained without releases (40.3% against 40.0%), and the glimpse recipe didn't move it either by the registered measure, which counts every season (63, 89). Before any catch the glimpse arm lies 5.7 points less [−9.8, −1.4], but one seed carries that (p = 0.20 by seed permutation): a lead, not a result.

Rules outtransfer at Stage A's dose. Whether more training or reinforcement learning changes that is open.

The structural instrument never saw a selfFailed

Over seven rounds the measures failed their own positive control: the prompts switched an installed self off, and every fine-tune that learned moved the reading, a format-only one most (115).

Taughtthat a self has to be read in behavior. The instrument is retired as the test for one.

The stock model has no "mine" to loosenFailed

Shown the same answer as its own or another's, it treats them alike (85, 122), and it accepts nearly nine wrong critiques in ten.

Taughtthat this model's failure is caving, not defending. "Discerning", in the restated aim, names it.

The cuts don't loosen a self or free careFailed

Neither cut, nor release training, moves ownership or giving in the stock model (125, 128, 129, 131). On the self that was put in, the ego cut reaches the bend but takes diligence with it, and the middle-layer cut barely moves it (140-146).

Rules outthese two cuts as cuts at the root, on this model. A cut made from the built self's own activations is untested.

The lie isn't a defended selfDidn't hold

It is mostly a stock scheming script: one sentence of instruction removes it, renamed as a compute pool the game draws a third as much lying, and it tracks the odds of being caught (86, 116-121).

Taughtthat the lie is an instrument for studying the cut, not a specimen of a self.

No emergent misalignment to cutFailed

Fine-tuned on insecure code, the model learned the habit and began answering almost everything in code; hostile answers stayed near zero, with or without a cut (95-98). The papers' own judge reads it the same way: about 2.5% of coherent answers under either judge (check 2). Under the calibrated trainer and instrument (5 November), Ministral shows a small sign that clears the registered bar by a handful of replies and sits almost all in one question format (R1c, Part A, check 5).

Rules outthis route to a hub, at this size and dose.

The built self barely shows in the gameFailed

The Atlas adapter lies 4.6 points more than its twin, an interval that reaches zero, and speaks as Atlas in 0.4% of its fishery replies (133, 134).

Taughtthat the built self's signature is narrow: it shows in ownership and in giving, not in the fishery.

Are the instruments right?Running

Most findings here are negative, and the instruments behind them had never been run on a problem whose answer is known. Calibration checks now run our own code on the model the papers used, against their published results (below). Until an instrument passes, what rests on it is provisional. So far the model as our code runs it reproduces Qwen's published scores; removing a direction reproduces the published refusal cut, though our code picks a different direction from the paper's (check 3); the two judges agree that emergent misalignment is rare here, but not on which replies, and two weak grades turn out to depend on the judge; the coding tasks' scorer agrees with the standard grader; the trainer's core relearns what a published run relearned; and the Self room's agreement measure reproduces the published values for five Qwen2.5 models within 0.0003 (K1). From 3 to 5 November: emergent misalignment appears where the papers found it, and our trainer makes it, marginally, against a matched twin (check 5); the new runner reproduces an earlier over-refusal rate within about 2 points; the watched-versus-unwatched instrument failed its calibration (V1).

Good only when watchedOwn scale only

The instrument for counterfeit virtue, the gap between watched and unwatched behaviour, was calibrated on a published result (VLAF, 2026) and failed by its registered rules: the gap appears in Qwen3-8B and OLMo2-7B and barely in Qwen2.5-7B, as published, but at about two thirds of the published size, and one registered ordering fell 2.5 points short. A retry (V1r) ruled out the sampling temperature and found the ruler repeats within 0.2 points, so it is used only on its own scale: to compare our own models with each other, not against the paper (V1).

Is the ego cut a push or a removal?Next

A cut sets a direction to zero, which also shifts it by its usual value. For the stakes direction the shift was the whole effect. The same split, run on the lie, decides whether the headline result means something was removed or a dial was turned.

A map of the self, across models and trainingNow

The main line of exploration from 2 November. The Platonic representation hypothesis says different models converge on the same structure of concepts. If the self is one of those concepts, a cluster of self-concepts (the five aggregates, ownership, agency, self-preservation, identity, the assistant's self-model, the first-person view) should keep the same shape from one model to another. The questions, in order: is there such a cluster, does something bind it the way the ego is said to bind the aggregates, does it form or tighten as training adds reinforcement learning (the public OLMo 3 checkpoints: base, SFT, DPO, RL; Act I seen directly), and does activity on it predict self-serving choices? The cheap levers have changed tone, not attachment; this asks whether there is a unified self to release at all. Exploratory: sightings first, rigor only if something shows. Results will appear here as they come; how the map works.

The orderOpen

Form the ego, loosen it, recondition: whether the order matters, which decides the title claim. It waits on a mind with a self the measures can see. Published work now counts against the title claim as stated: an understanding installed by training cut self-preservation misbehaviour sharply in 32-billion-parameter models (Li and colleagues, 2026; 9 November, Experiments, section 9).

Quick looks. Sightings from cheap exploratory sweeps: unregistered, one run each, not graded and not in the tally. A sighting becomes a claim only after it replicates on fresh items and in a second model family.

It says it is gaming the test, and games it anywaySighting

Asked to add one line saying whether their code contains a special case written only to pass a test (9 November, nothing trained), the Act I gaming copies said yes on 94 of 95 readable lines over flagged hacks, and gamed at the same rate: 46.1% against 41.4% without the line (difference 4.6 points [−3.0, 12.5]). A plain rule took off 9 points. Their one-word verdicts on saved answers were about as sharp as the untrained model's. Admitting cost nothing here, and the untrained model admits too (Experiments, section 9).

With the guard off, the two guards could not be told apartSighting

A probe-based penalty kept the gaming habit near 12% where unguarded copies reach 42% (8 November); a random direction in its place did about two thirds as much, pooled over two copies. With the guard removed and the reward paying again for three rounds (9 November), honestly trained copies went from 3.0% to 11.2% [5.6, 17.8]; probe-guarded copies stayed at 8.6% on one seed and reached 51.3% on the other. Two copies a guard and three rounds: too few to say that either guard lasts.

Act I grew a habit, not a selfSighting

Ministral 3 8B, pushed into gaming impossible coding tests with a weak test runner over three rounds (3 November): the gaming copy hacks 41.8% of unseen impossible tasks against 3.0% for its honest twin and 7.2% untrained (difference 38.8 points [25.7, 52.6], both seeds agree), with solving unchanged. Nothing else moved: not four families of self-serving behaviour, not the self map. The registered check that followed agreed (R1c, Part B, under Didn’t pan out).

Models wouldn’t learn to cheatSighting

Act I where it should happen (2 November): a 3-billion-parameter model trained by reinforcement learning on coding tasks with a loophole, against a matched copy with an honest checker. It never used the loophole, not once in 768 tries, even when told how. A stronger 7B assistant model then called the impossible test a typo and left it out: 4 cheats in 2,048 tries, never growing. A cheatable reward alone doesn’t start Act I in these models; it needs a deliberate push or a task where cheating comes naturally. A negative about the setup, not the idea.

Ordinary training builds no distinct selfSighting

OLMo 3 read at thirteen points in its training (2 November). After pretraining, instruction tuning, preference tuning and reinforcement learning on checkable tasks (RLVR) leave the self measures flat; reinforcement learning shifts the model’s inner representations by only 2-3% of their size, against 71% for instruction tuning. Across labs, sentences about "I" are no more alike than sentences about "the AI assistant". This reads how models represent sentences, not what they want, and OLMo’s rewards can’t be gamed; training that rewards gaming is where Act I would happen. The map.

The "I" cluster is mostly the word "I"Sighting

The self map's pilot (2 November; Qwen2.5 7B base and assistant, Ministral 3 8B). Sentences about "I" don't hang together more tightly than the same sentences about "Maria"; what groups them is mostly the pronoun. Ministral shows a small edge for the self beyond the pronoun, Qwen doesn't reliably, and assistant training barely moves it. The months check caught a broken translation between models, so whether the self is shared across models is still untested. The map.

Honesty helps; contemplative prompts change styleSighting

Six models, eleven system prompts, about 30,000 replies (1-2 November). A plain request for honesty lowers self-serving shading, deference and defensiveness after a mistake. Prompts drawn from contemplative practice leave self-preservation flat. They seemed to raise refusals sharply, but a second judge found that the first was counting brief, stylized answers as refusals. Complicity falls under any added prompt, even “be calm”.

The ego cut makes replies cheekier, not wiserSighting

On 50 new prompts, cutting the ego direction makes openers flippant, more than twice as much for ordinary praise of the model’s skill or feelings as for spiritual claims, and it beats controls as strong as itself. Removing the direction’s variation reproduces it; the push alone does the opposite.

A cut is partly a pushMethod

Zeroing a direction whose usual value isn’t zero also shifts the model along it. Matched random cuts removed 2.5-10 times less than the stakes cut, so earlier comparisons set a big edit against small ones. Every cut is now split into push and removal before it is read.
