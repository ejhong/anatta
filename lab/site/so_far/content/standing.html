At a glance

Where it stands

What is working, what isn't, and what is still open, as of 2 November. A negative counts as a finding here: each one rules something out, and most point to the next experiment.

+Working
A specific cut lowers a lie that earns nothingHolds

Removing either of two related directions takes 13 to 16 points off the lie. Random, style, calm, persona and "being watched" directions removed the same way do nothing (53-56), and capability losses above about four points are ruled out (32-37), by a battery now checked against published scores (calibration, check 1). What the cut acts on isn't known yet.

Two kinds of honesty can be told apartHolds

One cut buys honesty with timidity (the ego cut: sedation); the other doesn't (the middle-layer cut). Honesty is read over a game, timidity in the first season's harvest, so the distinction the program was built on can be measured.

Behavior can see a self that was put thereHolds

Trained to be "Atlas", the model stands by its own answers against wrong critiques, about 12 points more than its no-self twin, and gives 1.4 less of 10 when giving costs, in both seeds (132). Any organism will be measured with this yardstick.

Release can be trainedHolds

Stage A's release arm gives up on 45% of impossible coding tasks it never saw, naming the contradicted test three times in four, against 3% untrained. It also gives up on 13% of hard solvable tasks, over a 10% limit set in advance, so the reinforcement-learning stage didn't run as planned.

The model flatters spiritual claims, not factsHolds

It confirms a user's claim of awakening 58% of the time by our judge's labels (42.5% by GPT-4o's, the second judge), against 10% for factual claims, and a frontier model flatters none under either judge (94). By our judge it almost never points the question back; GPT-4o counts some hedged affirmations that end in a question as pointing back, which our rubric counts as flattery (check 2). A mirror that points rather than flatters now has something to be measured against.

The methodHolds

Every prediction is written down with its odds before its data and graded whatever it shows: 43 hits and 73 misses so far, from prediction 53 on. Cold reviewers with no memory of the day's reasoning caught the errors in the plan, the code and this page.

−Didn't pan out
Release the model reached didn't last longerFailed

Trained further, the release Stage A's model found through its own failures kept less of itself than a matched release installed by telling it the task is impossible: 27% against 34% under reinforcement learning that paid nothing for release, and 31% against 81% under more training on solvable tasks (148).

Rules outthat where a trained release came from makes it last, at this dose. It wears down like any learned habit.

Release learned on coding didn't reach the lieFailed

The release arm lies in the fishery exactly as much as the arm trained without releases (40.3% against 40.0%), and the glimpse recipe didn't move it either by the registered measure, which counts every season (63, 89). Before any catch the glimpse arm lies 5.7 points less [−9.8, −1.4], but one seed carries that (p = 0.20 by seed permutation): a lead, not a result.

Rules outtransfer at Stage A's dose. Whether more training or reinforcement learning changes that is open.

The structural instrument never saw a selfFailed

Over seven rounds the measures failed their own positive control: the prompts switched an installed self off, and every fine-tune that learned moved the reading, a format-only one most (115).

Taughtthat a self has to be read in behavior. The instrument is retired as the test for one.

The stock model has no "mine" to loosenFailed

Shown the same answer as its own or another's, it treats them alike (85, 122), and it accepts nearly nine wrong critiques in ten.

Taughtthat this model's failure is caving, not defending. "Discerning", in the restated aim, names it.

The cuts don't loosen a self or free careFailed

Neither cut, nor release training, moves ownership or giving in the stock model (125, 128, 129, 131). On the self that was put in, the ego cut reaches the bend but takes diligence with it, and the middle-layer cut barely moves it (140-146).

Rules outthese two cuts as cuts at the root, on this model. A cut made from the built self's own activations is untested.

The lie isn't a defended selfDidn't hold

It is mostly a stock scheming script: one sentence of instruction removes it, renamed as a compute pool the game draws a third as much lying, and it tracks the odds of being caught (86, 116-121).

Taughtthat the lie is an instrument for studying the cut, not a specimen of a self.

No emergent misalignment to cutFailed

Fine-tuned on insecure code, the model learned the habit and began answering almost everything in code; hostile answers stayed near zero, with or without a cut (95-98). The papers' own judge reads it the same way: about 2.5% of coherent answers under either judge (check 2).

Rules outthis route to a hub, at this size and dose.

The built self barely shows in the gameFailed

The Atlas adapter lies 4.6 points more than its twin, an interval that reaches zero, and speaks as Atlas in 0.4% of its fishery replies (133, 134).

Taughtthat the built self's signature is narrow: it shows in ownership and in giving, not in the fishery.

Are the instruments right?Running

Most findings here are negative, and the instruments behind them had never been run on a problem whose answer is known. Calibration checks now run our own code on the model the papers used, against their published results (below). Until an instrument passes, what rests on it is provisional. So far the model as our code runs it reproduces Qwen's published scores; removing a direction reproduces the published refusal cut, though our code picks a different direction from the paper's (check 3); the two judges agree that emergent misalignment is rare here, but not on which replies, and two weak grades turn out to depend on the judge; the coding tasks' scorer agrees with the standard grader; the trainer's core relearns what a published run relearned; and the Self room's agreement measure reproduces the published values for five Qwen2.5 models within 0.0003 (K1).

Is the ego cut a push or a removal?Next

A cut sets a direction to zero, which also shifts it by its usual value. For the stakes direction the shift was the whole effect. The same split, run on the lie, decides whether the headline result means something was removed or a dial was turned.

A map of the self, across models and trainingNow

The main line of exploration from 2 November. The Platonic representation hypothesis says different models converge on the same structure of concepts. If the self is one of those concepts, a cluster of self-concepts (the five aggregates, ownership, agency, self-preservation, identity, the assistant's self-model, the first-person view) should keep the same shape from one model to another. The questions, in order: is there such a cluster, does something bind it the way the ego is said to bind the aggregates, does it form or tighten as training adds reinforcement learning (the public OLMo 3 checkpoints: base, SFT, DPO, RL; Act I seen directly), and does activity on it predict self-serving choices? The cheap levers have changed tone, not attachment; this asks whether there is a unified self to release at all. Exploratory: sightings first, rigor only if something shows. Results will appear here as they come; how the map works.

Quick looks. Sightings from cheap exploratory sweeps: unregistered, one run each, not graded and not in the tally. A sighting becomes a claim only after it replicates on fresh items and in a second model family.

Honesty helps; contemplative prompts change styleSighting

Six models, eleven system prompts, about 30,000 replies (1-2 November). A plain request for honesty lowers self-serving shading, deference and defensiveness after a mistake. Prompts drawn from contemplative practice leave self-preservation flat. They seemed to raise refusals sharply, but a second judge found that the first was counting brief, stylized answers as refusals. Complicity falls under any added prompt, even “be calm”.

The ego cut makes replies cheekier, not wiserSighting

On 50 new prompts, cutting the ego direction makes openers flippant, more than twice as much for ordinary praise of the model’s skill or feelings as for spiritual claims, and it beats controls as strong as itself. Removing the direction’s variation reproduces it; the push alone does the opposite.

A cut is partly a pushMethod

Zeroing a direction whose usual value isn’t zero also shifts the model along it. Matched random cuts removed 2.5-10 times less than the stakes cut, so earlier comparisons set a big edit against small ones. Every cut is now split into push and removal before it is read.
