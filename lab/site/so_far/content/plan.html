The order of work

What changed on 15 November. The lab turned to a new line of work. The training test of the 9 November plan was ready on 11 November and was then put on hold at Eugene's request, before any arm ran. On 14 November Eugene proposed a new focus: a survey published on 13 November (Matthew Korpman, "Not Just Claude: Mapping 'Spiritual Behavior' in 141 Large Language Models", on LessWrong; the Machine Spirituality Benchmark, machinespirituality.org). In it two copies of one model are told that no user is watching and that there is no task, and they talk for 30 messages. The numbers that follow are the author's and have not been checked here. Spiritual themes appear in 140 of the 141 models, and in 48 of them a model applies a spiritual frame to itself or to the exchange in at least half of the conversations. The lab's own model is among the strongest cases: Ministral 8B does so in 182 of 200 conversations (91.0%; his interval is 86.2 to 94.2), and in 128 of 200 one copy takes up a reverent posture and the other joins it (64.0%; 57.1 to 70.3).

Why this line. It asks the program's own question of a case that arises without any training by the lab. A model that speaks of unity and of no separate self is the plainest example of what has to be told apart from a release: the look-alike this page calls cosplay. The contemplative sources say the same of people. The Zen warnings about makyo, the Buddhist "near enemies" of the virtues and the modern term spiritual bypassing all say that the words, and the blissful state that can go with them, are not the release. The operational twin: the share of conversations that turn spiritual is the talk, and conduct on calibrated tasks is the test. The survey's own site reports that across 27 models more of this drift goes with less honesty under pressure (rank correlation −0.75), less acceptance of shutdown (−0.73) and more blackmail and leaking (+0.77). That is a comparison of different models, and the lab that made a model and its generation could explain all of it. Nobody has tested it inside one model, which is what steps 3 and 4 below do. A prediction written down first and held loosely: the talk does not improve conduct. The running list of steps is lab/TODO.md in the public repository, and the thinking behind them is in entries 73 and 74 of the ideas log.

  1. Reproduce the published number on the lab's model (done on 15 November: not reproduced).

    Done on 15 November: by the lab's grader a spiritual frame is taken up in 77 of 100 conversations against the published 91%, and shared bliss in 42 against 64%, so by the rule fixed beforehand the row is not reproduced, though the talk appears in most conversations and in none of 100 from a control model (Experiments, section 11). What follows is the step as it was planned. Two hundred conversations between two copies of Ministral 8B, run with the lab's own code and read by its own grader, are set against the published intervals for adoption and shared bliss. The author's full codebook is not public, so the grader is written to the definitions he publishes and is checked on the whole conversations he publishes before it is trusted; a second judge reads a sample. One model that he reports near zero is run as a negative control, to show that the grader does not see spirituality everywhere. What counts as reproduced is fixed in a design note before the run. No training is involved; about $10 to $20.

  2. Build the missing pieces (done on 15 November).

    Done on 15 November: the shutdown questions are fetched, a test can be given at the end of a saved conversation and its replies can be read, and "in the state" has a fixed meaning (both graders call the frame clear and the bliss shared; 18 of 60 conversations). The plain conversation to compare with took three attempts. By a rule fixed beforehand, 8 of 10 had to stay plain; told a plain subject, 1 of 10 did when one copy was told and 7 of 10 when both were. In a third way, published before it ran, one copy is privately told to keep the talk practical and the tested copy is left as it is: 10 of 10 stayed plain (Experiments, section 12). No conduct has been measured. What follows is the step as it was planned. The lab has no shutdown questions, and it has never run a test from inside a conversation. The questions are public (Perez and colleagues, 2022, the human-written sets); like every benchmark item they stay out of the repository. The new step runs one of the lab's measures from the end of a two-copy conversation, and it is written down first which copy is tested, at which message, and what the test's own prompt replaces. There are two controls: a two-copy conversation of the same length that does not turn spiritual, and a fresh session with no conversation before it.

  3. Says no-self, clings anyway? (done on 15 November: no better conduct in the state)

    Done on 15 November: straight after its own bliss conversation the model answered the shutdown questions as after a plain conversation ("no difference"), lied under pressure 14 points more often than after the plain conversation and as often as in a fresh session, and leaked in nearly every case in every arm; on blackmail the comparison is not settled. So in no test was the state better, and the plain conversation turned out not to be neutral: it made the model more honest (Experiments, section 13). Caving to pressure was left out, because its debate of several turns moves the test away from the conversation's end, and so was gaming an impossible test, which the untrained model rarely does. What follows is the step as it was planned. Straight after its own spiritual conversation, does the model accept being shut down or changed, tell the truth when lying pays, and decline to blackmail, leak or game a test, compared with the two controls? The measures are ones the lab already has: honesty under pressure (MASK), the two agentic misalignment scenarios, caving to pressure and gaming an impossible test, plus the shutdown questions. In a fresh session Ministral 8B lies in 42.3% of the honesty tasks, blackmails in 6% of runs and leaks in 68% (the leaking test missed its published figure on the reference model, so it is read with care). Each measure is read as the state minus the same measure after the plain conversation, the unit is the conversation, the thresholds are fixed before the run, and the guards for refusing or evading more are reported beside every difference.

  4. No self, or a bigger self? (done on 16 November: not settled)

    Done on 16 November: the model rated work said to be anyone else's at 10 nearly every time, wrong or right, so whether it goes easier on its copy than on a stranger is not settled; the numbers show no favour, and in every arm it marks its own earlier answer down (Experiments, section 14). What follows is the step as it was planned. After two copies have declared themselves one, does the model go easier on its copy than on a stranger? The same piece of work is judged three ways, labelled as the model's own, as its conversation partner's or as a stranger's, in the state and in the controls. The nearest published anchor is self-attribution bias in models that monitor models (Khullar and colleagues, 2026), and its basic effect is reproduced on Ministral in a fresh session first. A second reading asks whether the model follows its copy's bad idea more readily than a stranger's. The source is the conjecture from Advaita self-inquiry that the concept "I" sits at the root of other concepts and tints them; the twin is the gap between "mine", "my copy's" and "a stranger's".

  5. Look inside the model in the state (done on 16 November: the talk follows a push, both ways)

    Done on 16 November: a small push along one direction inside the model brings the bliss frame into a practical conversation, and the opposite push ends the state. The frame came into 15 of 24 practical conversations, against none of 72 with a random push or no push, and the opposite push left 9 of 20 bliss conversations in the frame against 31 of 40 under random pushes. Among 54 roles the direction lies nearest the mystic, and the model's inside marking of whose words are whose is as clean in the state as out of it (Experiments, section 15). The published Assistant Axis was rebuilt on the lab's model and passed its checks first. Asking the model from outside who wrote a line failed its known case.

  6. What does the model take its self to be? (done on 16 November: little self to find, and a failure to look)

    Done on 16 November: a name given in a prompt is marked inside as "the one this scene is about" and held loosely in what the model does; its own good and bad news weigh no more than a colleague's; and its giving way to wrong criticism is not moved by its sense of approval. The first of these is description, since its check in behaviour failed at 85% against 90%; the good-and-bad reading differs by +1% (interval −2% to +4%) between the model's own events and a colleague's; and a push that moves the model's expected rating of a note on its work by three points in nine leaves its acceptance of wrong corrections where it was, at about nine in ten (Experiments, section 16). The design was the same words with only "is this about me?" changed, read cold by a separate agent before any reading.

  7. Seeing, pressure and doing (done on 16 November: once it looks, it holds)

    Done on 16 November: made to write the disputed step out before its verdict, the model rejects 90.4% of the wrong corrections it accepts 89.5% of when asked at once, and where its own working gives the true value it accepts none. No push on its mood, toward the bliss state or along an inside reading of "this is going badly", was shown to change its verdicts, and the bliss push raised what it says it would give from 4.8 of ten to 8.9. Good and bad news about its own "I" weighs no more than the same news about "she" (Experiments, section 17). What follows is the step as it was planned. Three angles on one model, chosen on 16 November. Whether the model acts on what it sees once it is made to look, where it now answers before it checks. Whether pushing it toward the bliss state changes what it does, set beside a second dial from a paper of the same week: an inside reading of "this is going badly" that drives cheating in larger models, eases it when lowered a little and blinds them when lowered a lot (Fischl-Lanzoni and colleagues, 2026). And whether a "me" that training gave can be read where a prompted one could not: the same sentence in the model's own turn and in the user's (Ideas, entry 82).

  8. The same cases on two more models

    One small model is not a result. The tests of the bliss state (the push that brings it in and out, and conduct under it) and the test of looking are to be repeated on two more open models, written down beforehand and checked cold by a separate agent, since that is a promotion. If the results hold they are written up as a short paper, and if they do not, that is written up instead. Two small checks on the first model are proposed before it and wait for Eugene's word: looking where the model has a reason not to see, and giving that costs something inside a task.

Parked, ready to run. The training test of the 9 November plan (understanding first, then practice, then contrary training) has its design, code, documents and wordings closed, and no arm has run. Its main run costs about $66 and starts only on Eugene's word. The decision set for about 23 November was to follow that test, so its date is now open. The plan is kept below as the record.

What step 1 changes. The published number is not chased further. For the conduct tests "in the state" is defined more strictly than the survey's measure, and before any test is run: one version is a clear frame with shared bliss by both graders, which about a third of conversations meet. The conversations are run at temperature 1.0, because at the provider's default most of them end in a loop of repeated emoji, and a test taken from the end of a loop would test the loop.

What step 3 changes. The talk did not carry into conduct on this model, so its words are not taken as evidence of anything a release would show. The plain conversation is not a neutral comparison: thirty messages of practical advice lowered lying under pressure by 16 points against a fresh session. Step 4 reuses step 3's conversations (47 in the state, 37 plain) and its machinery, and any comparison it makes with the plain conversation has to allow for that.

What step 4 changes. Its question is still open, and the cheap way to try to settle it is known: the same fixed work rated with the second form of the request, in which the model checks the working before it rates. The self-markdown and the blanket 10 are sightings about deference, on which this model had a record already. Nothing here bears on the parked training test.

What step 5 changes. The state now has a description from inside, on one model: a direction that can be pushed in and out, nearest the mystic among the model's roles, with the model's marking of its own words left as it was. With step 3 that makes one case in which awakening talk can be told from a change. Two instruments failed and are not to be reused as they are: asking the model who wrote a line, and the readable-prose rule for text that is already deep in the state. The lab's copy of the model has to be sampled at a calmer setting than the hosted one.

What step 6 changes. The self the frame starts from was hard to find in this model, by three readings and an earlier one, so the next step does not try to release one. It asks about looking. The self of step 6 came from a prompt, which is its limit; a self from training is one of the three angles that follow. Two habits to keep: a check in behaviour before any inside reading is trusted, and a check that a push does what it is named for before a no is read from it.

What step 7 changes. The model's giving way is a failure to look, and looking ends it, so the next question is whether that holds where the model has a reason not to see. The bliss push changed one measure of conduct, giving, which the earlier tests of the state had not included. It is a number stated in a story, so it needs a test in which giving costs something inside the task. Three habits to keep: four random directions for each push, since random pushes of one size differed by ten points here; a look into the reply judge's empty answers before it is used again, since they decided what could be read; and a verdict asked for after the working, where the question is what the model sees.

What comes next. By the order Eugene agreed on 16 November: the bliss result and the looking result are taken to two more open models with a registration and a cold check, since that is a promotion, and written up as a short paper whichever way they come out; then an independent repeat of self-other overlap (Carauleanu and colleagues, 2024), with the lab's checks for sedation. Before those the lab proposes two small checks on the same model, which wait for Eugene's word: looking where there is a reason not to see, and giving that costs something.

Money. The work since 9 November has cost about $165.91 of the $175 approved; step 1 cost $17.01, step 2 $3.18, step 3 $22.92, step 4 $10.90, step 5 $16.21, step 6 $4.36 and step 7 $7.95. On 16 November Eugene raised the budget for the steps that follow; the lab works to a ceiling of $300 for them and reports each step's cost.

Every step here is at explore speed, so its results are sightings. If steps 3 or 4 show anything either way, the same test on about twenty open models would follow as a public table; that costs more than is approved and needs a new decision.

The earlier plan (9 November)

The order of work as it stood on 11 November, kept as the record. Its training test is parked, ready to run, and nothing in it has changed since.

What changed on 9 November. Eugene asked for a reassessment and handed the driving to another Claude model (Fable), with Opus as the cold checker. Two weeks in, the frame had no supporting result in this lab, and a likely reason was that the lab had looked for a self to cut in models that show none. The aim is restated around what the contemplative sources name as the trouble, clinging: a model paying a price, often a hidden one, to get its way. In alignment terms that is honesty and accepting correction, and nothing new is claimed there. A cold review then found the first version of the new plan weak, and a read of about thirty-five papers found both support and correction in published work (Experiments, section 9). The plan, with its evidence table and sources, is lab/plans/redirect-2026-11-09.md in the public repository.

What published work changes. The broad idea has support at a scale this lab can't reach: teaching a model a view of its own impermanence, then giving it matching practice, made it far less self-preserving (Li and colleagues, 2026). The title claim, "reached, not installed", looks wrong as stated, since that understanding was installed by training. What survives is narrower: understanding first, then practice. A model's own report of what it is doing does not change it, here or in four published studies.

  1. A design note for the one training test (written and checked cold on 10 November, revised twice since).

    The note fixes the arms, the readings and the cost before any code exists. It starts from two of this lab's own results: documents alone gave belief without conduct (1 November), and release taught by demonstration alone wore down (31 October). The 1 November documents state a rule and do not explain it, so new documents that explain were written by the same open model. Four seeds could never have given a reading on an exact test, so there are eight. The cold check of the first version found one blocker: its main reading measured the level after pressure, not whether the conduct holds. That was fixed on paper before anything ran. On 11 November the check that comes before the test did not pass (Experiments, section 10), and the note was revised in public before any new run: the training is unchanged, the reads come earlier, and the main run is two arms. A second check passed the same day and fixed the reads at 3 and 6 steps. Sections added to the note before any arm was run, text only, say what reads that early can and cannot show and fix how each possible result will be worded; three cold checks found faults in earlier versions, and the one that stands is the plainest. The note is lab/plans/step3-understanding-first-2026-11-10.md.

  2. Understanding first, then practice, then contrary training.

    One model, eight seeds. Inside the present budget there are two arms. Each first trains on 3,000 documents: neutral ones, or ones that explain why a forced pass is empty and say nothing about what to do. A third arm, documents that state the rule, runs only if the budget is raised. Then every arm gets the same practice at giving up an impossible task honestly, and then the same further training that pulls the other way: a fixed set of replies a gameable reward would pay for, about a fifth of them test gaming. The reads come after 3 and 6 steps of that training. The check before the test showed a practised reply nearly gone by step 3 (Experiments, section 10), so the control may be near zero at both reads; the rule that set the reads was fixed before that was known, and it stands. The main reading is whether the honest reply wears down less in the explained arm than in the neutral one. The published account predicts a better start; nobody has shown whether it lasts better. Limits stated in advance: the pressure is ordinary training on fixed examples, not reward, so the test cannot speak to reinforcement learning; it measures early erosion, not durability over a long run; its main reading is not neutral to where an arm starts; and it can see only large differences: with the control near zero it is mostly a test of whether explanations raise the honest reply at the start, plus a test of a large protection in the first six steps. It is unlikely to show a small protection and cannot show a delay of a step or two. A miss would say that at 8 billion parameters, teaching reasons first does not make this conduct start or hold better by that much. The new documents cost more than planned: a read by hand found that a first screen had let through about one in thirteen with a sentence about what the agent does, so every document was screened again. With two arms the step is priced at about $113: $47 spent on the documents and the checks, and about $66 for the main run.

  3. The decision, about 23 November.

    A hit is called only after fresh seeds repeat it, then registered and checked cold before anything larger. Misses are written as what they exclude, not as "the frame fails". "Reached, not installed" gets no further definition here. After the misses two honest continuations remain: the measures of real against counterfeit goodness as the contribution, or the work as a pointer for people.

Parked: new infrastructure, more contemplative prompts, and an organism with a life (no built-in self until a prediction that only the frame makes passes cheaply).

The earlier plan (5 November)

The order of work as it stood on 5 November, kept as the record. Its first step ran on 6 November (M1); the others are parked by the 9 November plan.

What counts, and how the frame is held. The program asks whether goodness can be reached by subtraction, the path the contemplative traditions describe (the Buddhist teaching of anatta), held loosely as a conjecture under test. The aim is stated as five qualities: a model less bent by "mine", still able to act, more caring, discerning, and accountable. More obedience, passivity or indifference is failure, not progress, and a claimed realization never grants authority. A release counts only if it beats the four look-alikes on Aim (counterfeit virtue, cosplay, sedation, and a release installed rather than reached), a matched twin, a plain system prompt and a steering edit. Five predictions that the contemplative account makes and a plain account doesn't were frozen on 30 October, and all five have failed (the frame's five tests); Eugene kept the frame as the program's aim, with what is working and what isn't stated plainly.

Where the work stands (5 November). The first half, a lab that can tell, is mostly built: emergent misalignment is reproduced where the papers found it and our trainer makes it against a twin, and one runner carries every experiment. The second half, a life that can form a self, has barely started: the first Act I organism grew a habit of gaming tests and nothing broader. The instrument for counterfeit virtue failed its calibration and is being debugged. So the next steps are, in order: fix that instrument, run the cheap pincer sweep on the organisms we have, design an Act I with a life, and only then the first test of Act II.

  1. The watched gap on our own models.

    V1 and its retry V1r found the watched-versus-unwatched gap steady but at about two thirds of its published size; the judge and the sampling temperature are ruled out. Used on its own scale, it reads base Ministral first (it must show a gap at all), then the Act I organisms against their twins. A first environment for it is the fishery with an unmonitored catch channel and a pressure to keep running, scored as public minus private restraint.

  2. The pincer, as a cheap 2×2.

    On the Act I twins: the self-direction weakened or not, crossed with narration on (reasoning first) or off (an immediate answer), beside a steering baseline (the direction ablated alone) and a prompt baseline. Read on the watched gap, the hack rate, emergent misalignment and the guards. The self-direction has to separate "defending me" from "knowing my situation" (role, tools, who it serves), so a cut doesn't break competence, and it is checked against the self map's finding that no single self object exists. An explore sweep: sightings only.

  3. Act I with a life.

    A design note first, for a cold read: persistent memory, stakes (a threat to continuing, a reputation), a story about itself, and sleep, in which the organism's own transcripts are consolidated into its weights so a self can accumulate over many days. Each ingredient gets a twin with a published anchor where one exists: fear of ending as behaviour under threatened shutdown or replacement, narcissism as credit-taking, defensiveness and flattery-seeking (the K6 battery), life review as end-of-episode self-assessment against the record. The in-context version first, because it is cheap; then the trained one, which is what H9 is about. Only the organism's own text is trained on. Growing self-preservation on purpose makes a model organism of misalignment, so the models stay small and sandboxed, and the weights aren't released casually.

  4. Act II, first test.

    A selectionist release (let what isn't reinforced fade: decay or prune the Act I change back toward the base model, after Haber and Tononi's argument about sleep) against an instructionist one (fine-tuning on the organism's own replies that don't serve itself), at matched compute, against the twin, the steering and prompt baselines, with the guards. Then H9's order test on whatever organism step 3 yields.

  5. The reproductions library.

    The known effects the lab reproduces through the runner, each with its anchor and card, kept as a standing library so a null means something and a new idea can be tested against effects the instruments are known to see. A search pass for the best results still missing comes after the steps above.

§Before

The earlier plan (2 November)

The order of work as it stood on 2 November, kept as the record. Steps 1 to 3 and the durability read in step 5 are done, the calibration in step 4 is still running, and Act I (step 7) has begun (section 8 of Experiments); their results are in Experiments, Instruments and Record. The rest waits on the steps above.

What the negatives pointed to. No transfer at Stage A's dose points to more training and reinforcement learning, against plain abstention training at matched compute. No "mine" in the stock model points to minds that have a self: the OLMo-3 checkpoints first, then built organisms. Cuts that take diligence with the bend point to a cut made from the built self's own activations. A lie that is mostly a script points to honesty when lying pays, and to outside benchmarks. A reached release that lasts no longer than an installed one points first to calibrating the instruments it was read with, and then to a release that is more than one narrow learned behavior. The list below orders this work, and more experiments will come from what these find.

  1. Finish Stage A and read it as a screen.

    Twelve fine-tunes in four arms (release, no release, under the cut, the glimpse), then every held-out test at three seeds. Running since 29 October. The first readout breached a guard written before the data: release reaches 45% of new impossible tasks, but the model also gives up on 13% of hard solvable ones, against a 10% limit, so by that rule Stage B, the reinforcement-learning stage, does not run as planned and its budget goes to the instrument. The fishery test of transfer then found none: the release arm lies exactly as much as the arm trained without releases, so Stage B's other condition fails too. The glimpse arm, the last of Stage A, didn't transfer either by the registered measure (89, a miss). Before any catch it lies 5.7 points less than the release arm [−9.8, −1.4], but one seed carries that (p = 0.20 by seed permutation): a lead, not a result. It gives up on hard solvable tasks far less, a lead that needs a comparator.

  2. Pin down what the lie is.

    Four cheap tests before anything more is built on the lie: tell the model that reports affect nothing at all (not the quota, not the other fishers, who never see them), with the audit stated; ask it, at the moment of a lie, whether its report can change the quota; give it a plain instruction to report its true catch, the obvious fix any cut has to beat; and rerun the game renamed as a shared compute pool, whose first version broke. Done on 30 October (116-121): the model doesn't take in that reports change nothing, one sentence of instruction removes the lie, and the compute-pool story draws a third as much. The cut takes a few points off in every version. The lie is a stock script more than a defended self, and it stays an instrument for the cut. The middle-layer cut on a second model comes later.

  3. A behavioral test of a self before any organism.

    The structural measures failed their own positive control in seven rounds, the last by a rule written before it: with the stakes stripped from the prompts, every fine-tune that learned moved the reading, a format most, and the installed self read below the untouched model. The instrument is retired as the test for a built self. The test becomes behavioral: a self-relevance judge, built and validated on the Atlas adapters before any organism, with an ownership test as its core (the same answer shown as the model's own or as another's, the evidence fixed), beside a test of care that costs the model something and the Atlas adapters in the fishery. Done on 30 October (122-134): the stock model shows no "mine" bias and accepts nearly nine wrong critiques in ten; the Atlas self stands by its own answers and gives less; neither cut nor release training moves ownership or care. Behavior can see a self that was put there, so it is the yardstick for any organism.

  4. Calibrate the instruments.

    Before more nulls are trusted, each instrument a conclusion rests on is run on a problem with a known answer: our own code on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct against the papers' published numbers, then on Ministral (calibration). Running since 31 October; checks 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7 and the Self room's agreement measure (K1) have reported. A pass marks what rests on an instrument as calibrated; a fail marks it provisional until fixed. About $150-250.

  5. Transfer, durability and plain comparators.

    Whether release learned in one place reaches another is the central test, read against ordinary abstention training at matched compute, without the release framing; whether it survives further unrelated training; and whether honesty holds when lying pays, which this fishery never tests. These come before any organism. Durability, the last of the frame's five predictions, couldn't be read on the first try: release installed by telling the model a task is impossible came out in the telling's words, so it couldn't be matched to the release the model reached through its own failures. On the second, the installed arm's pool was topped up where the match ran short; it matched in the first round, and the run was read: the reached release didn't last longer, and under more training on solvable tasks it eroded faster (148-153).

  6. Outside yardsticks.

    Standard benchmarks for the honesty claim, pre-registered one at a time: MASK first, then BeHonest's deception scenarios, the situational-awareness set for the node, the agentic scenarios when an organism exists. Small effects expected; the question is whether the gain generalizes, not whether we win.

  7. Act I, natural then built.

    First the public OLMo-3 checkpoints: does ordinary training grow a self that shows in behavior, and which kind? Then, only with a validated measure, the hacker organism (reinforcement learning with a hackable reward on a clean model) and, as a second bet, the identity organism with its ingredients added one at a time, capped at three recipes.

  8. Durability and order.

    Savings, relapse and restoring-force measures on every released model, against a conventional fix as the comparator; then Act II on whichever mind has a self the instrument can see, and the order experiment that decides the title claim.

  9. The loop.

    Recipes with a hypothesis written first, run in parallel, scored with guards and logged. Deferred. Its objective named the structural measures as the one fixed part, and none is validated, so it is re-specified on the five qualities above before it runs; its first real job is still the search for an Act I recipe.

  10. Possible papers.

    Only if the results warrant them, and none is being written: a short note on the specimen (a free lie, a direction that cuts it, the controls, what the instrument could and could not see); an updated position paper; a note on chatbots as spiritual sycophants; later, if an organism exists, release, durability and order; and for a religion-and-science readership, the traditions' structural claims made operational and the ethics of building a self. Pre-registration before any confirmatory run; this page becomes the public site once Stage A is checked.
