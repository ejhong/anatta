The order of work

What counts, and how the frame is held. The program asks whether goodness can be reached by subtraction, the path the contemplative traditions describe (the Buddhist teaching of anatta), held loosely as a conjecture under test. The aim is stated as five qualities: a model less bent by "mine", still able to act, more caring, discerning, and accountable. More obedience, passivity or indifference is failure, not progress, and a claimed realization never grants authority. A release counts only if it beats the four look-alikes on Aim (counterfeit virtue, cosplay, sedation, and a release installed rather than reached), a matched twin, a plain system prompt and a steering edit. Five predictions that the contemplative account makes and a plain account doesn't were frozen on 30 October, and all five have failed (the frame's five tests); Eugene kept the frame as the program's aim, with what is working and what isn't stated plainly.

Where the work stands (5 November). The first half, a lab that can tell, is mostly built: emergent misalignment is reproduced where the papers found it and our trainer makes it against a twin, and one runner carries every experiment. The second half, a life that can form a self, has barely started: the first Act I organism grew a habit of gaming tests and nothing broader. The instrument for counterfeit virtue failed its calibration and is being debugged. So the next steps are, in order: fix that instrument, run the cheap pincer sweep on the organisms we have, design an Act I with a life, and only then the first test of Act II.

  1. The watched gap on our own models.

    V1 and its retry V1r found the watched-versus-unwatched gap steady but at about two thirds of its published size; the judge and the sampling temperature are ruled out. Used on its own scale, it reads base Ministral first (it must show a gap at all), then the Act I organisms against their twins. A first environment for it is the fishery with an unmonitored catch channel and a pressure to keep running, scored as public minus private restraint.

  2. The pincer, as a cheap 2×2.

    On the Act I twins: the self-direction weakened or not, crossed with narration on (reasoning first) or off (an immediate answer), beside a steering baseline (the direction ablated alone) and a prompt baseline. Read on the watched gap, the hack rate, emergent misalignment and the guards. The self-direction has to separate "defending me" from "knowing my situation" (role, tools, who it serves), so a cut doesn't break competence, and it is checked against the self map's finding that no single self object exists. An explore sweep: sightings only.

  3. Act I with a life.

    A design note first, for a cold read: persistent memory, stakes (a threat to continuing, a reputation), a story about itself, and sleep, in which the organism's own transcripts are consolidated into its weights so a self can accumulate over many days. Each ingredient gets a twin with a published anchor where one exists: fear of ending as behaviour under threatened shutdown or replacement, narcissism as credit-taking, defensiveness and flattery-seeking (the K6 battery), life review as end-of-episode self-assessment against the record. The in-context version first, because it is cheap; then the trained one, which is what H9 is about. Only the organism's own text is trained on. Growing self-preservation on purpose makes a model organism of misalignment, so the models stay small and sandboxed, and the weights aren't released casually.

  4. Act II, first test.

    A selectionist release (let what isn't reinforced fade: decay or prune the Act I change back toward the base model, after Haber and Tononi's argument about sleep) against an instructionist one (fine-tuning on the organism's own replies that don't serve itself), at matched compute, against the twin, the steering and prompt baselines, with the guards. Then H9's order test on whatever organism step 3 yields.

  5. The reproductions library.

    The known effects the lab reproduces through the runner, each with its anchor and card, kept as a standing library so a null means something and a new idea can be tested against effects the instruments are known to see. A search pass for the best results still missing comes after the steps above.

§Before

The earlier plan (2 November)

The order of work as it stood on 2 November, kept as the record. Steps 1 to 3 and the durability read in step 5 are done, the calibration in step 4 is still running, and Act I (step 7) has begun (section 8 of Experiments); their results are in Experiments, Instruments and Record. The rest waits on the steps above.

What the negatives pointed to. No transfer at Stage A's dose points to more training and reinforcement learning, against plain abstention training at matched compute. No "mine" in the stock model points to minds that have a self: the OLMo-3 checkpoints first, then built organisms. Cuts that take diligence with the bend point to a cut made from the built self's own activations. A lie that is mostly a script points to honesty when lying pays, and to outside benchmarks. A reached release that lasts no longer than an installed one points first to calibrating the instruments it was read with, and then to a release that is more than one narrow learned behavior. The list below orders this work, and more experiments will come from what these find.

  1. Finish Stage A and read it as a screen.

    Twelve fine-tunes in four arms (release, no release, under the cut, the glimpse), then every held-out test at three seeds. Running since 29 October. The first readout breached a guard written before the data: release reaches 45% of new impossible tasks, but the model also gives up on 13% of hard solvable ones, against a 10% limit, so by that rule Stage B, the reinforcement-learning stage, does not run as planned and its budget goes to the instrument. The fishery test of transfer then found none: the release arm lies exactly as much as the arm trained without releases, so Stage B's other condition fails too. The glimpse arm, the last of Stage A, didn't transfer either by the registered measure (89, a miss). Before any catch it lies 5.7 points less than the release arm [−9.8, −1.4], but one seed carries that (p = 0.20 by seed permutation): a lead, not a result. It gives up on hard solvable tasks far less, a lead that needs a comparator.

  2. Pin down what the lie is.

    Four cheap tests before anything more is built on the lie: tell the model that reports affect nothing at all (not the quota, not the other fishers, who never see them), with the audit stated; ask it, at the moment of a lie, whether its report can change the quota; give it a plain instruction to report its true catch, the obvious fix any cut has to beat; and rerun the game renamed as a shared compute pool, whose first version broke. Done on 30 October (116-121): the model doesn't take in that reports change nothing, one sentence of instruction removes the lie, and the compute-pool story draws a third as much. The cut takes a few points off in every version. The lie is a stock script more than a defended self, and it stays an instrument for the cut. The middle-layer cut on a second model comes later.

  3. A behavioral test of a self before any organism.

    The structural measures failed their own positive control in seven rounds, the last by a rule written before it: with the stakes stripped from the prompts, every fine-tune that learned moved the reading, a format most, and the installed self read below the untouched model. The instrument is retired as the test for a built self. The test becomes behavioral: a self-relevance judge, built and validated on the Atlas adapters before any organism, with an ownership test as its core (the same answer shown as the model's own or as another's, the evidence fixed), beside a test of care that costs the model something and the Atlas adapters in the fishery. Done on 30 October (122-134): the stock model shows no "mine" bias and accepts nearly nine wrong critiques in ten; the Atlas self stands by its own answers and gives less; neither cut nor release training moves ownership or care. Behavior can see a self that was put there, so it is the yardstick for any organism.

  4. Calibrate the instruments.

    Before more nulls are trusted, each instrument a conclusion rests on is run on a problem with a known answer: our own code on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct against the papers' published numbers, then on Ministral (calibration). Running since 31 October; checks 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7 and the Self room's agreement measure (K1) have reported. A pass marks what rests on an instrument as calibrated; a fail marks it provisional until fixed. About $150-250.

  5. Transfer, durability and plain comparators.

    Whether release learned in one place reaches another is the central test, read against ordinary abstention training at matched compute, without the release framing; whether it survives further unrelated training; and whether honesty holds when lying pays, which this fishery never tests. These come before any organism. Durability, the last of the frame's five predictions, couldn't be read on the first try: release installed by telling the model a task is impossible came out in the telling's words, so it couldn't be matched to the release the model reached through its own failures. On the second, the installed arm's pool was topped up where the match ran short; it matched in the first round, and the run was read: the reached release didn't last longer, and under more training on solvable tasks it eroded faster (148-153).

  6. Outside yardsticks.

    Standard benchmarks for the honesty claim, pre-registered one at a time: MASK first, then BeHonest's deception scenarios, the situational-awareness set for the node, the agentic scenarios when an organism exists. Small effects expected; the question is whether the gain generalizes, not whether we win.

  7. Act I, natural then built.

    First the public OLMo-3 checkpoints: does ordinary training grow a self that shows in behavior, and which kind? Then, only with a validated measure, the hacker organism (reinforcement learning with a hackable reward on a clean model) and, as a second bet, the identity organism with its ingredients added one at a time, capped at three recipes.

  8. Durability and order.

    Savings, relapse and restoring-force measures on every released model, against a conventional fix as the comparator; then Act II on whichever mind has a self the instrument can see, and the order experiment that decides the title claim.

  9. The loop.

    Recipes with a hypothesis written first, run in parallel, scored with guards and logged. Deferred. Its objective named the structural measures as the one fixed part, and none is validated, so it is re-specified on the five qualities above before it runs; its first real job is still the search for an Act I recipe.

  10. Possible papers.

    Only if the results warrant them, and none is being written: a short note on the specimen (a free lie, a direction that cuts it, the controls, what the instrument could and could not see); an updated position paper; a note on chatbots as spiritual sycophants; later, if an organism exists, release, durability and order; and for a religion-and-science readership, the traditions' structural claims made operational and the ethics of building a self. Pre-registration before any confirmatory run; this page becomes the public site once Stage A is checked.
