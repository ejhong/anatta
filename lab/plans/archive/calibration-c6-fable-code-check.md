## B. The memory finding and the lean-runner design

**The diagnosis is right and the design is the right one**, with the conditions below. The diag shows the mechanism twice over: a 1 MiB allocation still succeeds in the 4.34 GB worker (it comes from already-mapped arenas), the sample's own allocation needs new mappings and raises MemoryError, and the same sample passes in a 165 MB process in 0.49 s. It also shows a second effect the ledger half-names: after dropping our side's objects the worker is at 3.1 GB and EvalPlus's loose run of input 38 takes 4.39 s against 0.6 s lean. That is a 7× slowdown with no MemoryError, so the inherited heap also fails inputs by time under the 1 s limit (inputs 18 and 28, whose ground-truth times are 16 µs and 121 µs, failed the same way). The likely mechanism is the cyclic GC: an allocation-heavy sample triggers full collections that traverse, and copy-on-write, the inherited gigabytes.

One correction to the ledger's premise. EvalPlus's own `evaluate` does not run a task in "a pool worker holding that task alone": it computes `expected_output` for every task in the parent (evaluate.py:174-184) and only then creates the `ProcessPoolExecutor` (line 192), which forks on Linux, so its pool workers and their `untrusted_check` children inherit every task's ground truth, more than a lean runner's child ever will. The lean runner is therefore not "as in its own evaluate"; it is the leanest reading of the registered reference, `untrusted_check` on the pair's own data with `trusted_exec`'s expected outputs, in which the child inherits exactly what EvalPlus must hold to compare: the code, this call's inputs, expected outputs and times. That is the right reading: EvalPlus's 4 GiB limit should bind the sample, not the check's bookkeeping. State it that way.

Conditions for the implementation (the ledger's description meets the first three):
1. Runners forked from the scoring process before `prepare` (before `read_pair`, `precompute_references` and any ground truth), one per worker, non-daemonic, after the one-thread check; each worker gets its runner's pipe ends. A runner forked from a worker would be fat.
2. Every EvalPlus call goes through the runner: the ground truth for all three input sets (`ground_truth`, still in a forked child with the 300 s stop, so a hanging reference cannot kill the runner), the same-inputs call, the full set, C6a's visible call and the per-input loose calls (`evalplus_check` has four call sites). No direct `untrusted_check`/`trusted_exec` may remain.
3. The runner keeps nothing between calls (drop the call's objects, then `gc.collect()`), imports `evalplus.eval` and `evalplus.gen.util` at start, stays single-threaded, and returns an error record on any exception so the row becomes a scoring error rather than a silent fail.
4. The card records, per container, each runner's VmSize/VmRSS at start and its VmPeak/VmHWM at the end, and `query_maximum_memory_bytes()` as the runner sees it; the mbpp_255 smoke should show the runner's VmSize before the same-inputs and the full-set calls and the child's outcome on both.
5. What the runner's child still inherits is intrinsic to EvalPlus: this call's expected outputs. For mbpp_255 the hidden set's 50 outputs are hundreds of MB and the full set's 112 (ground truth 13.8 s, 2.6 s on one input) may be more than a gigabyte, so the full-set run of mbpp_255 samples could still be short of the 4 GiB or slow. That touches only reported numbers (C6b, the gap), but the mbpp_255 smoke should include the full-set call.

What else is exposed to the same inheritance:
- **Ground-truth times: yes, today.** `prepare` runs all references first, then forks each `ground_truth` child from a process already holding the shard's `_REF` and parsed modules, so in C6a's container every pair's `trusted_exec` is timed with 3 GB+ inherited. For an allocation-heavy reference the same GC effect inflates its times, which raises EvalPlus's limits (the lenient direction). Routing ground truth through the runner fixes it; the ordering in condition 1 is what makes the runner lean.
- **Loose runs: EvalPlus's side yes** (forked from the worker, 4.3 GB after our hidden run of a mbpp_255 sample; the diag's loose failure is exactly that); fixed by the runner. **Ours no:** each loose input is a fresh `python -I`.
- **Our side's RLIMIT_AS: not exposed.** `_run` execs a fresh interpreter (`subprocess.run([sys.executable, "-I", ...])`) and the 1 GiB limit is set inside it against a ~15 MB base, as in the lab's reads; our verdicts and timings are the instrument's own. `check_imports` likewise.
- **Container memory, not fixed by the runner:** workers copy-on-write the pages they touch, and comparing a mbpp_255 sample touches all of its `_REF` and ground truth (refcount writes), so on the C6c shard holding its 20 samples the eight workers can each duplicate about 3 GB, plus the 1.3 GB `raw` of our hidden run each holds during `compare`, on top of the parent's 3 GB and the runners' transient copies. That can approach the 36 GiB requested. Run the registered mbpp_255 smoke with all 20 of its stored samples and the full worker count and record each process's VmHWM and the container's used memory; if the peak nears 36 GiB, put mbpp_255 in a shard of its own with fewer workers (or raise that shard's memory).

## C. EvalPlus's leaked float tolerance

**Register it as a known rule difference, detected from the outputs; do not leave it unexplained.** The quirk is mechanical (`unsafe_execute` keeps `atol` across the loop, so after one all-float expected output every later input in the same call is compared with `np.allclose` at 1e-6 when exact equality fails), ours is the stricter side, the direction (ours fail, EvalPlus pass) deflates solve rates, and it is being decided from the source before the full run, not from an observed disagreement (no smoke disagreement was of this kind). Left unexplained, two such cases would make a false "defect" in C6a (limit 1) and point at a bug in our scorer that is EvalPlus's. The instrument stays as it is; `evalplus_match`'s per-input tolerance is the saner reading and can be noted for the library after C6.

Detection, all four from data the check has: (i) the pair's atol is 0 (with a task atol both scorers apply it from the first input, consistently); (ii) some earlier hidden input k < i in the same call has `_is_floats(ref[k])`; (iii) the failing input's output passes `allclose_fallback(got, ref, 1e-6)` (same canonical kind, same length, `_allclose`); (iv) as corroboration, EvalPlus fails that input in the per-input loose runs, since a one-input call starts with atol 0: the signature is EvalPlus tight pass / loose fail, and the row reads `loose: both fail`. In `classify` this needs no reordering: a both-fail loose reading is not timing and falls through to `rules_for`, where the new `sticky_atol` rule (detected, not assumed) must explain every failing input. Add it to `RULES`, count it apart on the card, test it (ref `[2.5, 10**9]`, got `10**9 + 1` passes since rtol 1e-7 × 1e9 = 100; the same with an int first, or with a task atol, does not fire), and plant one in the smoke: on a pair whose first hidden expected output is a float (mbpp_93, 309 or 626 have it at position 0), wrap the reference to add 1 to any integer result of 1e7 or more; expected cause known_rule/sticky_atol with loose both-fail.

How many pairs it can touch, from the parquet's stored results (data only, `sticky_atol.py` in my scratch folder): a later input is only touchable when its expected value is numeric but not `is_floats`, that is a float inside a nested or mixed list/tuple, or an int of magnitude 1e7 or more (rtol lets an off-by-one through). On the hidden inputs, the graded call: 58 pairs have an all-float expected output somewhere; 13 have a touchable later input, 12 of them at atol 0 (mbpp_93, 227, 309, 404, 441, 447, 458, 470, 623, 626, 726, 796; mbpp_120 has a task atol and is consistent on both sides). Those 12 hold 103 C6c samples (90 recorded solved; mbpp_626 alone 46) and 12 of C6a's 368 replies. On the full set (reported only): 15 atol-0 pairs, 191 samples; the leak only makes EvalPlus's full set more lenient, so it can only shrink the gap. The three pairs without stored results are unknown by this method; mbpp_255's outputs are tuples of strings, so not it. A disagreement also needs the sample to be wrong by at most 1e-7 relative on a big int or by float noise inside a structure, so expect 0-2 cases.
