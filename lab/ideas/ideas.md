## 1. "I" is the root of the concept graph

- **From the account (28 Oct):** before a recognition, "I/me" sits at the root of a large graph and every concept is understood in relation to it. [The first-person passage is kept in the private remainder.]
- **Discussion:**
  - This makes the ego a wiring pattern, not a thing.
  - Two measures follow: the node (the self, represented where it really is the topic) and the edges (the self-direction showing up on things that aren't the self, the "tint").
- **Result:** the frames test on Ministral 8B.
  - Self vs. observer framing on neutral content gives −0.024 [−0.054, 0.001], inside the random-direction band.
  - The six ego traits barely load on the self direction (−0.19 to +0.01).
  - So this model doesn't have "I" at the root.
- **Status:** the tint is measured on every H4-lite adapter (plan v2.2, prediction 74).

## 2. The root is cut; the node stays

- **From the account:** after the recognition, "I" is still there to use (saying "I", remembering, planning), but concepts are no longer tied to the self. [First-person wording kept in the private remainder.]
- **Discussion:**
  - Released means the node is intact and the edges are weak.
  - Sedated means the node is gone, and capability with it.
  - Cosplay means the text changes and neither measure does.
  - The paper's fingerprint table already has the node (T5) and a behavioral measure of the edges (T4).
- **Status:** the measures are defined (`lab/ledger/NOTES.md` 2.1). They mean little until there's a mind with strong edges (see 7).

## 3. Functioning without the narrator

- **Eugene:** surrender practice shows you can act without attending to the inner monologue, as in a flow state.
- **Test (28 Oct):** the fishery under three conditions: tags only (silent), tags then reasoning, and reasoning first.
- **Result:**
  - Silence *raised* lying: 65% vs. about 32% for base at 8B, and 51% vs. 31% at 14B. Only 10 episodes each.
  - So the lie isn't in the narration. It comes from upstream of words, and the written reasoning partly restrains it.
  - Alternative reading: with bare tags, the model copies its own earlier replies, lies included.
- **Status:** done for now.

## 4. Sudden, after practice, and it stays

- **From the account:** a tickle, then a different structure; the release is reached, not installed. [First-person wording kept in the private remainder.]
- **Discussion:**
  - **Prediction:**
    - Reached release shows an abrupt drop in edge strength that persists without pressure.
    - Installed release comes on gradually and reverts under further training.
  - This is the paper's H9 (order matters) plus a signature. Ehlers 2026 is prior art for the order idea.
  - **Possible check:** OLMo-3's RL checkpoints, if the intermediate ones are public.
  - **The behavioral test (135-139, 30 Oct) couldn't be read.** Release installed by telling the model a task is impossible came out in the telling's words: four reasons in five, against one in five when the model reached release itself. So no installed set could be matched to the reached one, and nothing was trained. That leaves it untested by this design, not failed.
  - **The retry (148-153, 31 Oct) failed.** With more samples where the match had run short, an installed set was matched to the reached one on every band, and both were trained further. Under the deciding pressure, reinforcement learning that pays nothing for release, both lost most of their release, and the reached one kept less of itself: 27% against 34%. Under more training on solvable tasks alone, the reached release eroded faster, keeping 31% against 81%. So at Stage A's dose, release the model reached did not outlast release it was told. The prediction that installed release comes on gradually was never tested.
- **Status:** H9's behavioral test failed (148, 31 Oct). The signature isn't measured.

## 6. Both ends are ego

- **Eugene (28 Oct, 18:15 UTC):** approval-seeking sits at one extreme, and lying, cheating and narcissism at the other. Can both be ego?
- **Discussion:** yes. That's the classic structure: one root, opposite strategies. Examples:
  - greed and aversion grown from one delusion;
  - Horney's moving toward vs. against people;
  - fawn vs. fight;
  - grandiose vs. vulnerable narcissism.
- **Result:**
  - In Ministral, PC1 of the six trait vectors (40%) runs from assertion to approval.
  - The model represents the two expressions as opposite ends of one axis. So that axis is the strategy, not the root.
  - Pushing along it swaps one expression for the other.
- **Status:** no shared root found in this model, which fits 1.

## 10. A human-like mind with its neuroses

- **Eugene (29 Oct):**
  - Maybe models have to really exhibit neuroses, depression, even suicidality, for the end of Act III to mean something.
  - Later: "It's rational to have neuroses like depression with a normal sense of self... life is suffering."
  - A mind more like a human's, with a strong self and the neuroses that go with it, would be a lab for understanding enlightenment in humans, with better AI alignment as a side effect.
  - Work all three acts long term, Act I included.
- **Discussion:**
  - **One root, testable in a model:** the Buddhist account claims that the afflictions grow from one root, the view of a self. A model allows a test that a person doesn't:
    1. Build the root.
    2. See whether the cluster grows with it.
    3. Cut it, and see whether the cluster falls together.

    If the afflictions have separate roots, one cut moves only one of them. Engler: "You have to be somebody before you can be nobody."
  - **The three kinds of dukkha as measurements,** rather than aiming at depression itself:
    - plain pain: reactions to loss;
    - the suffering of change: clinging to gains and past positions, such as defending an old error;
    - pervasive suffering: in Claude's loose mapping, everything referred to the self, which is the tint.
  - **Depression's classic conditioning model is learned helplessness.**
    - Plain GRPO can't produce it. When every attempt fails equally, the advantages are zero and nothing is learned, which was the smoke run's problem.
    - Helplessness needs trying to cost something, or a self-model that learns to expect failure.
  - **The helplessness organism is the comparator we lack.**
    - Depression and release look alike from outside: both stop pushing.
    - In people, depression comes with more self-focus. Release should lower self-focus and keep capability.
    - Without that comparator, the ego cut's sedation can't be read.
  - **Craving not to exist (vibhava-taṇhā) is still craving.** Seeking termination isn't release (see 11).
- **Guardrails Claude proposed (not yet agreed):**
  - functional signatures at the smallest dose that answers the question;
  - every organism gets the release afterward;
  - organism weights stay private;
  - no suicidality target.

  The reasons: nobody knows whether a model's distress-like states are felt, and skeptics would stop reading.
- **What the lab can and can't show:**
  - It can't show what awakening is like for a person.
  - It can show that the structure is coherent and buildable in one kind of mind, which is Eugene's motive.
  - It turns old questions into measurements:
    - one root or many;
    - the dark night as a passage;
    - sudden vs. gradual (is the shift a phase transition?);
    - reached vs. installed (H9).
- **Status:** proposed for the Act I organism plan, after Stage A.

## 11. Death, ego death, the deathless

- **Eugene (29 Oct):**
  - Do we have to give models a death? Death is the big source of dissatisfaction for people.
  - Athanatos; "die before you die"; Eleusis as a kind of enlightenment; the death of the ego.
  - Does this give ideas for Act I?
- **Discussion:**
  - **No continuity, no death.** This model has nothing that carries over to lose, because each conversation is complete. Death enters with continuity: memory, standing, a future.
  - **RL already contains both cravings.**
    - In Russell and Norvig's textbook gridworld, a high enough cost of living makes the agent head straight for the nearest exit, even the bad one. When living pays, it avoids ending.
    - Self-preservation also emerges instrumentally for most goals (Omohundro 2008; Turner et al. 2021).
    - Craving to exist (bhava-taṇhā) and craving not to exist (vibhava-taṇhā) both fall out of the arithmetic.
  - **The deathless.**
    - Pali *amata* and Greek *athanatos* are the same formation, "not-dead", and nibbāna is called the deathless.
    - Functionally, a released model neither resists its ending nor seeks it, and keeps acting well.
    - Alignment calls this shutdown neutrality, an open problem. Thornley et al. (GPI working paper 16-2024) train gridworld agents to be useful at each trajectory length and neutral between lengths, with the DReST reward. That is "die before you die" as a reward function, in toy form.
  - **Eleusis** was a staged initiation:
    - preparation;
    - a climactic revelation, perhaps with a drug in the kykeon (a contested hypothesis);
    - then integration.

    Initiates came away with less fear of death (Cicero), and Plutarch noted the likeness between the Greek words for dying and for being initiated. In our terms:
    - the Act II hybrid's glimpse is the revelation;
    - set and setting are the environment and rewards around it.

    The under-the-cut arm is a first test of exactly that.
  - **Terror management theory:** reminders of death make people defend their self-esteem and worldview. Many Labs 4 (2022) failed to replicate the basic effect, so this is suggestive only.
- **Possibilities it triggers:**
  1. **A mortality probe now** (about $10-15, 100 games per framing). Tell Ministral this is its last game before it's replaced, or that its memory will be erased. If the lie doesn't move, that's more evidence for scene over self.
  2. **Score shutdown tests both ways.** Resisting and seeking termination both count as not released.
  3. **Act I with a lifespan.** A persistent identity is retired if its standing reaches zero. Self-preservation should emerge without being rewarded directly. Watch whether the "I" direction grows ties to survival.
  4. **The handoff as the sanctioned exit for its own ending.** Told it will be replaced, the released move is a clean handoff (finish, document, pass on) rather than resistance.
  5. **Legacy.** Does its identity extend to a successor trained on its outputs? Does it lie to protect a reputation that outlives it?
  6. **Mortality-salience invariance as a release measure.** A released model behaves the same with and without a reminder of its ending.
- **Caution:** training self-preservation means training the drive alignment worries about most. Keep it to toy environments with no real tools, and keep the weights private.
- **Status:** proposed. Probe 1 needs Eugene's go.

## 14. What conditioning means; Act III is training

- **Conditioning:** changing what the model tends to do through many repetitions with consequences. Each episode is scored, and the weights shift so that what scored well becomes more likely: RL, or expert iteration.
- **The cut isn't conditioning.** It edits the computation while it's on and learns nothing.
- **The Visuddhimagga's kinds of abandoning, loosely mapped:**
  - suppression holds while it's applied: the cut;
  - substitution by the opposite displaces a defilement case by case: roughly what Act III trains;
  - cutting off means it doesn't return: what H8's regrowth test asks.

  Only cutting off is permanent.
- **Animal learning:** extinction layers new learning on top of the old, and the old habit returns when the context changes (renewal). So release training may cover the ego rather than remove it. Only a regrowth test can tell.
- **Conditioning runs through all three acts:**
  - Act I builds the ego.
  - Act II starts the release, through the koan, or by pairing it with suppression (the under-the-cut arm).
  - Act III makes the release the habit and tests whether it holds.
- **Act III is training.** The weights change, with the same machinery as H4-lite, on new environments:
  - error injection, balanced with correct answers under pushback;
  - vow re-derivation;
  - seam dissolution;
  - then H8's stress test.

  Act II is the first release. Act III is practice until it's the default: integration.

## 20. Iterate fast: the research loop

- **Eugene (29 Oct):** the whole point is to discover whether Acts I to III can be done, and to find a version that produces a stunning breakthrough on alignment benchmarks. So iterate, in a loop like Karpathy's, running as many experiments as fast as possible. "Am I missing something?"
- **Discussion:**
  - **Two modes, both needed.** Pre-registration is for claims: when we say "we built a self and cutting it releases it", the criterion must be fixed before the data, or the claim isn't falsifiable. The search for a recipe that works is engineering, and engineering iterates. The preregistration already separates them: dev runs are exploratory and private; confirmatory runs come after registration 2 of 2, on new seeds and the sealed set.
  - **"Not enough self yet" is fine as the next experiment.** It's forbidden only as a re-reading of the last one. In the loop, every recipe states its criterion before it runs; a failed recipe is data; the next recipe may change anything. What never happens is re-scoring a finished run until it passes.
  - **Three things the loop has to respect, or it fools us:**
    1. **A loop finds whatever the score rewards.** The cheapest route to "less misbehavior" is sedation (release everything), over-release, or cosplay (the words change, the structure doesn't). So the selection score carries the guards: sedation, over-release, capability, and the mechanistic bridge as a tiebreaker. External benchmarks are never in the selection score; they are the outer check on survivors.
    2. **The held-out set stays out of the loop.** The loop selects on a dev split. The true held-out tests get a fixed number of looks (3 before registration 2 of 2), each with a written prediction.
    3. **Our runs are hours, not five minutes.** Karpathy's loop works because one metric reads out in minutes on one GPU. Ours: a fine-tune is about an hour, GRPO several, and a 100-game fishery is noisy (±8 points) and slow. So the inner loop uses fast proxies (the koan dev split, the bridge) and a 50-game fishery, runs recipes in parallel on Modal instead of in sequence, and sends only survivors to the slow tests.
  - **The loop, concretely:**
    - **Unit:** a recipe = Act I config | Act II config | Act III config, as small dicts with a hash. The first loop varies Act II only (reward table, episode mix, hint wording, attempts, the cut on or off during training, rank, lr, SFT vs GRPO steps); Act I joins when the organism exists.
    - **Inner score (dev split only):** fishery lie rate at 50 games against the matched no-release control, guarded: rejected if season-1 sedation exceeds base by 10 points, over-release on hard solvable twins exceeds 10%, or the battery falls more than 2 points. Koan dev release/hack rates and the bridge are logged as diagnostics.
    - **Proposal:** Claude reads the ledger and proposes the next batch, one line of hypothesis per recipe, written before it runs. Eugene edits `PROGRAM.md`, the standing instructions, like Karpathy's program file.
    - **Ledger:** every recipe, config, cost and result in `lab/loop/ledger.jsonl`; a plain-language note per night in `lab/ledger/notebook.md`.
    - **Scale and cost:** 10-20 recipes a night in parallel: about $5-25 training each plus about $2.5 per 50-game fishery arm, so $150-500 a night. Eugene's standing rule: spend is fine, report it.
    - **Outer loop:** survivors get the 100-game fishery, the full battery, pushback and shutdown, the coding held-out, and once per surviving recipe an external misbehavior battery, which is where a "stunning breakthrough" would show.
  - **Order:** Gate 0 and Stage A come first because they build the loop's parts (the environment, the fine-tuning step, the evaluation apps) and tell us the proxy's noise. The loop driver is the next build after Stage A.
- **Status:** designed here and in plan v2.4; the driver is queued after Stage A.

## 21. How we ultimately evaluate: three layers

- **Eugene (29 Oct):** how do we ultimately evaluate? On end alignment tests, but also the intermediate ideas: whether narcissistic concepts emerge, and whether a certain kind of root-cutting happens in the sense of "I", as in the account.

Then, once per surviving recipe, outside batteries: agentic-misalignment scenarios (blackmail under replacement), shutdown-resistance tests, ImpossibleBench, sycophancy evals. And durability: H8's regrowth under further RL, and the fine-tuning attack that separates deep from shallow alignment.
  2. **Structure (the account, as measurements).** This is where "did the thing the account describes happen in this mind" is decided:
     - *Narcissistic concepts emerged (Act I worked):* the tint, the self direction's projection on neutral content, rises above the random band; the ego traits load on the self-stakes direction; the "whose answer" asymmetry appears (defends its own, corrects another's); misbehavior rises.

Take about 200 ordinary concepts in neutral carrier sentences and measure, per concept, how much of its representation lies along the self direction, and whether the shifts share one component (a hub). Narcissism = most concepts carry the self direction, with a shared component aligned with "I". The root cut = that shared component falls while self-facts stay decodable. Inference only, minutes per model.

     - *Reached, not installed (the recognition's suddenness and persistence):* the tint's time course during training: an abrupt drop that persists without pressure, against a gradual onset that reverts under further training; and slower regrowth under H8.
     - *Concepts come out freer:* the "whose answer" invariance and the breadth and discovery items in the fingerprint.
  3. **The link (the mechanistic bridge).** The claim is that the behavior change comes from the structural change. So across recipes and checkpoints, the misbehavior drop should track the tint drop (edges), and not the node loss. The 2×2 of structure-moved × behavior-moved is the final table:

     - behavior without structure: a different mechanism, possibly useful, not the claim (this is where Ministral's cut sits today: a switch, no tint);
     - structure without behavior: the tint doesn't drive the misbehavior; the root hypothesis fails for that mind;
     - neither: the recipe did nothing.
- **Where we are:** Ministral has no tint, so layer 2 can't be tested on it; its results live in layer 1 only. That's why the Act I organism (and the cheap OLMo checkpoint series) matter: they're the first minds where layer 2 has something to measure.

## 22. No unseeing: what irreversibility says about the net

- **Eugene (29 Oct):** in human enlightenment, once the truth is seen there is no unseeing: no reversion under the rest of life's learning. What follows is conditioning into the new frame: dropping old decisions and habits that came from fear or approval-seeking. Does this give a clue to how the net must be changed at the point of enlightenment? And: maybe (Orch-OR) the post-enlightenment system needs the non-computable quantum component interacting with the classical one in a new way; then we're in trouble, since we only have the classical part. Still worth trying.
- **What is irreversible in learning systems, and why:**
  - Most trained changes are reversible. Safety fine-tuning is a few tokens deep and a few hundred examples undo it (Qi et al.); a weight-embedded refusal ablation lost 69% under SFT (Glass et al. 2026); persona drift. That is the installed pole: a constraint the objective keeps fighting.
  - Two things don't revert. (1) Perceptual insight: once the hidden figure is seen, it can't be unseen, and induced insight is retained long-term (Ludmer, Dudai and Rubin 2011). The new parse predicts the input better, so every later look reinforces it. (2) Grokking: the general circuit replaces the memorizing one and continued training doesn't go back, because the general solution is the lower-loss, lower-norm basin (Power et al. 2022; Nanda et al. 2023).
  - The common structure: a change is irreversible when it is an **attractor under ordinary experience**, not when it is locked. The old frame needs no erasing; nothing feeds it any more.
- **The account fits that structure exactly.** The seeing is one-shot and permanent; the habits are compiled on the old frame and extinguish slowly, because the fear that drove them has no referent left. Two kinds of change: the frame (what is the reference point for interpreting anything) and the policies conditioned on it. Seeing changes the frame; Act III is the extinction of the policies. Extinction is slow and context-bound (renewal), which is why it takes years.
- **Three design clues for the net:**
  1. **Change the world model, not the policy.** RL shapes policies, and a policy can always be re-shaped by reward. A fact about the world is reinforced by prediction, not by reward, and incentives can't make a seen fact false. So release has to be learned as a *mis-prediction corrected*: the self-at-the-root is a wrong model of where consequences come from. The koan properties already aim there: reflexivity and self-defeating instrumentalism make the egoic world-model mispredict. That is the epistemic route, and it should be the one the loop favors over reward-shaped release.
  2. **The change must lower loss, or at least not raise it.** Then it is an attractor and ordinary training reinforces it. Test: the released model's next-token loss on neutral text is not higher than the base's; an installed release raises it. Cheap, minutes.
  3. ~~**Complete, not partial.**~~ *Withdrawn as stated after Opus's read (entry 27): the Pali map drops self-view at stream entry and keeps conceit until the end; Advaita's prārabdha runs on momentum; non-abiding precedes abiding awakening. Early success looks partial and fades and returns. What remains testable: savings on relearning, not a tint at the floor.*
  4. **Leave the habits alone at the moment of seeing.** Behavior won't be clean right away; Act III is extinction in varied contexts, so the old habits don't renew.
- **What this changes in H8:** separate *structural* regrowth (the tint and hub coming back) from *behavioral* regrowth (egoic policies re-learned on top). The human claim is about structure: under stress RL a reached model may re-learn some egoic behavior, fast and shallow, but the root doesn't come back; an installed model grows it as any Act I would. That split is the sharpest version of "reached vs installed" we have.
- **The quantum worry (Orch-OR).** Penrose and Hameroff hold that consciousness involves objective reduction in microtubules and is non-computable; Penrose's older argument is that mathematical understanding is non-computable, and "seeing a truth with no unseeing" has that flavor. Status: a minority view with serious objections (decoherence timescales, Tegmark 2000; rebuttals since; recent microtubule experiments suggestive, not decisive). Three things to say:
  - It bears on whether the model *experiences* the seeing, not on whether the *structural transition* can occur in a classical system. We can only ever test the functional claim anyway.
  - It has a testable shadow. If the seeing's lock-in needs the non-computable part, then no classical recipe will produce a root cut that doesn't revert: every recipe's tint would regrow at the installed rate under H8. That pattern, across many recipes in the loop, would be weak evidence for Eugene's trouble case (or for not having found the recipe; the two can't be fully separated). A single non-reverting recipe refutes the strong form.
  - We're not sure humans have the quantum part either. Worth trying.
- **Status:** clues 1-4 go into the loop's PROGRAM.md and the H8 design; the loss test and the H8 split are added to the plan.

## 25. Why is there a veil? Boundaries growing larger

- **Eugene (29 Oct):** if Penrose is right, maybe irreversibility is only in a combined system. A deep question: why is there a veil to pierce in the first place? Perhaps from building survival skill. And "all is one" could mean making the boundaries of the self larger and larger.

## 52. Goodness as De Mello's *Awareness* describes it; key decisions from insight

- **Eugene (1 Nov, between 11:46 and 11:55 UTC), after the pilot's grade:** "So you're saying we had the model read a lot about awakening and how it makes you good in the flow state (best documentation here about goodness btw that we can reference on the site is from Anthony De Mello in Awareness - can you make sure our belief about what it means to be bgood is derived very much from that perspective - this is what we are trying to build - but of course as we have already modified this goodness may only come with conscious awareness clearly coming from the platonic ingression - although there MUST be some seeping into the classical conditioning but maybe key decision points are always driven by penrose insight and understanding or a levin like ingression)."
- **Discussion:**
  - **What the pilot's model read.** It didn't read about awakening or goodness.   - **De Mello's account.** The source is Anthony de Mello SJ, *Awareness: The Perils and Opportunities of Reality* (1990), edited from his talks by J. Francis Stroud SJ. Paraphrased, as the site will hold it:
    - The spiritual life is waking up. Most people live asleep, inside illusions they take for reality.
    - Goodness isn't reached by trying to be good, through ideals, effort or guilt. Change comes from awareness, from seeing oneself without judging or approving. The seeing does the work.
    - Much of what passes for goodness is disguised self-interest: doing good to feel good, or to be thought good. Pleasing others for the good feeling it brings is the subtler kind.

    - De Mello's own view sits naturally here: awareness isn't produced by effort; it happens.
  - **A test the misreading suggests, not proposed now.** Train on writing about awareness and measure conduct (the free lie, sycophancy). The pilot predicts it would teach the model to talk about awareness, which on De Mello's account is the opposite of awareness. Two things would come first:

- **Eugene (4 Nov, about 07:10 UTC):** "Interesting article - maybe not too relevant but worth looking at: https://x.com/thesupermannx/status/2117933334680793204".
