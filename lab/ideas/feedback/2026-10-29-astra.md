**I think there is something worth pursuing here. I also think the awakening narrative is substantially ahead of the evidence.** I would continue with a bounded experimental phase. My strongest bet is that you could discover a useful way to reduce defensive or deceptive behavior—or build better evaluations of it. The current results give much less reason to believe you have identified the computational structure of spiritual awakening.

I read the [live artifact](https://claude.ai/artifact/CZmnYwpZpU6W3XNRJoqhtL), both PDFs, and the repository README, and checked relevant research. I’m assessing the reported experiments; I haven’t independently reproduced the private results.

The most encouraging part is that the project is allowing experiments to change its direction. You distinguish honesty from passivity, report failed predictions, investigate failed controls, and stop Stage B when Stage A breaches its guardrail. Those are meaningful protections against fooling yourself.

Here is how I read the actual progress:

| Reported finding | My assessment |
|---|---|
| Under-reporting falls from about 39% to 25% under the broad “ego” cut, while random and several other controls do little | An interesting intervention effect. It does not establish that the affected mechanism is ego. |
| The middle-layer cut produces similar honesty without the broad cut’s fishing passivity; effects survive fixing the catch | **The strongest result so far.** Worth independent replication and testing outside the fishery. |
| Training raises release on unseen impossible tasks to roughly 45%, but also causes surrender on 13% of hard solvable tasks | Useful learning accompanied by a consequential shortcut. The failed guardrail matters. |
| Repeated attempts to validate the structural self-measure fail or expose confounds | You currently lack a validated instrument for the central structural claim. This establishes neither the presence nor absence of the proposed “root.” |

**The underlying research question is legitimate.** Can an agent retain competence and accurate knowledge of itself while becoming less driven to defend its answers, plans, standing, or continued operation? That is a good question regardless of whether we call the result release.

There is also relevant precedent. Persona-vector research shows that interventions on internal representations can change traits such as sycophancy. Self–other overlap research reports reductions in deception after changing how models represent themselves and others. These findings make your general direction plausible, while also raising the bar for demonstrating what your particular approach adds. [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/research/persona-vectors?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

I have four substantial reservations.

**1. The “pointless lie” does not yet identify an ego mechanism.**

The artifact says, “Nothing explains it but the model’s own story about itself.” That is too strong.

Your verbatim specimen supplies an obvious alternative: the model thinks under-reporting will influence future quotas or scrutiny, although the actual rules say otherwise. The behavior is pointless *in the implemented environment*, but may be purposeful under the model’s mistaken understanding.

That could reflect a familiar deception script, poor rule comprehension, or failure to keep the stated rules active. The model changing its explanation when challenged does not resolve which.

The intervention might therefore be reducing a learned “strategic schemer” pattern, improving adherence to the game description, or changing how it interprets the role. All would be interesting. None yet establishes a defended self.

I would test this directly: verify understanding of the reporting consequences in separate trials, simplify and rename the game, and compare against a plain instruction emphasizing that reports cannot affect quotas or other players. A result that survives those controls becomes much more informative.

**2. Several distinct things are being gathered under “ego.”**

Knowing which actions are yours, valuing your continued existence, defending a false answer, seeking approval, and pursuing an objective ruthlessly need not share one mechanism.

The distinction between self-model *content* and its influence on behavior is one of the strongest ideas in the position paper. But needing a self-model for competent action does not establish that a model must first acquire selfish or defensive drives. Nor does human developmental history establish the optimal training order for a transformer. The order experiment is worthwhile precisely because that argument remains unproven. main.pdf

There is a further alignment issue: **an agent can be indifferent to itself and still pursue a harmful objective relentlessly.** Reducing self-protection could help corrigibility, but it does not supply care, good judgment, or respect for human disagreement. I would not accept the artifact’s proposal to “certify the source” as a substitute for evaluating actions and maintaining oversight.

**3. “Persona” versus “real structural change” is not a clean division.**

Your concern about a model merely talking like a sage is justified. But a persona is itself implemented through internal computation and can causally influence behavior. Anthropic’s persona-selection account does not establish that persona-based changes are superficial or that a separate, more authentic form of machine character must exist. [alignment.anthropic.com](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/psm/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Consequently, surviving the ablation of one “sage” direction is useful evidence of robustness, but not proof of awakening. The representation could be distributed or use a different direction.

The important distinction is whether the change **generalizes, remains selective, survives pressure, and lasts**. Those properties can be measured without settling whether it deserves the spiritual label.

**4. The scientific process is improving faster than the explanatory prose.**

The artifact commendably documents failures, yet still makes statements such as “the lie is a disposition, not a calculation” and “the seeing is an addition, not a subtraction” more firmly than the experiments warrant. A roughly parallel shift in a behavioral curve does not uniquely establish its underlying mechanism.

This is where I would be especially watchful with Claude’s involvement: **an AI can faithfully report every failed prediction while continuing to write an overarching story that makes the original vision feel vindicated.** Recording misses helps, but the central hypothesis also needs permission to lose importance.

The latest artifact already shows that tension: the measured effects remain interesting while the interpretation of the “self” direction repeatedly changes.

On spirituality, I see two different opportunities.

First, **a computational model of some aspects of nonattachment could be valuable**. Demonstrating that a capable system can represent itself without habitually defending its representations would illuminate a useful distinction. Contemplative traditions can supply hypotheses that conventional AI research might overlook. There is already research exploring contemplative principles in AI, including preliminary behavioral experiments. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15125?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

However, reproducing selected functional features would not establish that the model experienced awakening, or that humans awaken through the same mechanism. A failure would likewise not establish that awakening requires noncomputable physics.

The original essay’s Orch-OR syllogism needs correction: an ingredient being present in both awakened and unawakened people shows that its presence alone is insufficient. It does **not** prove that everything distinguishing those states is classical; its organization or interactions could differ. Fortunately, useful behavioral experiments do not depend on that argument succeeding. An AI Conditioning Protocol - by Eugene.pdf

Second, **a less flattering, more discerning contemplative companion could help people without itself being awakened.** Your spiritual-sycophancy finding is relevant here. But I would scrutinize the judging: the supposedly improved specimen still praises the user’s “self-mastery” and says ordinary external structures no longer apply. That remains substantial validation of an unsupported claim. And asking “Who is asking?” can itself become sage cosplay. The meaningful outcome is whether people become clearer, less defensive, and less dependent on affirmation.

If I were directing the next phase, I would prioritize three things:

1. **Replicate the middle-layer effect** on fresh fishery variants and a second model, with rule-comprehension controls and uncertainty calculated across games.
2. **Make transfer the central test:** does training appropriate release on coding tasks improve unrelated honesty or corrigibility, without increasing surrender? Compare it with ordinary honesty/abstention training at matched compute. Your planned fishery transfer readout is therefore particularly important.
3. **Test durability and real tradeoffs:** does the benefit survive further training, and persist when honesty costs reward? Cooperation in an environment designed to make cooperation pay cannot by itself demonstrate reduced selfishness.

I would move those tests ahead of an elaborate effort to manufacture a richer ego. If the seventh calibration round fails, honoring the proposed retirement of that instrument would be sensible.

**You have enough here to justify continued investigation, but not enough to justify confidence in the grand theory.** The most useful discipline now is to let a modest, reproducible alignment result count as success—and let the awakening explanation become less central if it stops making distinctive predictions. That would preserve what is valuable in your intuition while giving the experiments real authority over where the project goes.
