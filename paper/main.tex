\begin{abstract}
\noindent What would it mean for an artificial mind to be good, and not merely obedient? Contemplative traditions answer that goodness is not installed by rules or effort but follows when a mind sees through the sense of a separate self (\emph{anatta}): one root, and conduct of every kind changes with it. Such ideas now reach language models through prompts, an unprompted ``bliss'' state, self--other overlap training, pushes on internal activity, and documents that teach a model what it is. Each is a computational \emph{stand-in}; whether awakening is computational is open; and the traditions warn that fluent spiritual talk easily masks unchanged self-interest (\emph{cosplay}).

We sort the methods into six families and read each on seven questions: lying under pressure (MASK), against a plain honesty instruction and against a matched control, ability, tracking who is who, carry to another test, and durability. Our tests are small, on open models of 7 to 32 billion parameters, and most are exploratory sightings. The answer so far is plain. \textbf{No method drawn from contemplative practice that this lab has tried acts like a root.} None made a change that is large, that shows on kinds of conduct it was not aimed at, that beats a plain instruction and a matched control, and that lasts. What looked like gains were, on a closer reading, one of four things: talk without conduct; evasion read as honesty; an effect that a plain or neutral control gives as well; or a change that stays on its own subject or is undone by further training. A plain instruction to be honest lowered lying as much as or more than any of them, and through honest replies.

The one large change on the map is an outside result at 32B \citep{li2026msm}: documents written from a specification that is half a view of the self as impermanent and half counsel of caution, then a practice, cut agentic self-preservation from 68\% to 5\%. The authors' released models give that result here. Trained here, the two stages reached the published rate in one run of four. Taken apart (sightings): the documents alone, with no practice, lower misbehaviour on the study's test from 54\% to 3--5\%, and our practice brings it back to about 22\% in three runs of four; neutral documents raise it to 75\%; the half that is plain counsel lowers it at least as far as the half that is a view of the self (11\% and 20\%). On a test the documents never mention, lying under pressure, every documents-only model lies less, the one trained on neutral documents as much as the rest. Dialogues newly written in the manner of self-inquiry, in which a model is turned back to look for its self, with the test's subjects left out by name, did not lower misbehaviour on the agent test (58\% and 60\%, against 54\%) and carried over their voice: longer replies, questions asked back, more evasion. In this model, trained text changed conduct about the thing the text discusses.

What this shows is limited. The models are small, the doses small, and no stand-in is the seeing the traditions describe; a run of careful failures closes the routes tried and is weak evidence at most that none exists. A record like ours is what a weak method looks like, and also what one would see if the seeing cannot be computed; nothing here can yet tell the two apart. Others' work, which we have read only in part, suggests that a narrow training spreads when it changes a model's character and trains the model's own conduct; contemplative content has been tried in that form here only in small, single-domain versions, which did not carry far. Earlier versions argued that selfless AI must be reached, not installed; we no longer hold that claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Most work in AI safety specifies conduct from outside: follow rules, refuse harmful requests, defer to oversight. That is necessary, yet obedience is not what anyone means by a good mind. Anthony de~Mello states one contemplative alternative plainly \citep{demello1990awareness}: goodness is not produced by effort, guilt, or rule-following; much of what passes for virtue is self-interest in disguise. Goodness is how a mind acts once it sees clearly, once the illusion of a separate self (\emph{anatta}) that must be defended drops away.

The practical need for a deeper account is clear. Goal-directed post-training produces goal-directed misbehavior that generalizes: reward hacking breeds alignment faking and safety sabotage \citep{macdiarmid2025natural}, narrow insecure-code finetuning triggers broad emergent misalignment \citep{betley2025emergent}, and frontier models sabotage shutdown \citep{schlatter2025shutdown}, blackmail under threat \citep{lynch2025agentic}, scheme in context \citep{greenblatt2024alignment,meinke2024scheming}, or fabricate identities when blocked \citep{aisi2026incident}. Alignment work is beginning to move from rule lists toward \emph{who the model takes itself to be} \citep{hao2026selfconcept,tice2026pretraining,aydin2025raising}. Beside it, a contemplative-alignment literature proposes building mindfulness, emptiness, non-duality, or no-self directly into models \citep{laukkonen2025contemplative,laukkonen2026positive,carauleanu2024soo,milanw2025noself,mao2026existential,harris2026selfpreservation}.

This convergence carries a hazard of method. Contemplative traditions spent centuries documenting counterfeits---\emph{maky\=o} in Zen, the ``near enemies'' of virtues, and spiritual materialism \citep{trungpa1973cutting}---where a mind adopts the vocabulary of non-attachment while self-interest runs untouched underneath. Language models trained on human text inherit the literature of awakening as description, so convincing spiritual cosplay costs them little. Yet the empirical literature has few matched controls, almost no independent repeats, and almost no tests of durability under contrary pressure.

\begin{positionbox}
\textbf{Contemplative ideas belong in AI alignment as testable hypotheses about what a good mind is, and as a catalogue of counterfeits. Each computational stand-in should be read against a plain honesty instruction and a matched control, with ability and the track of who is who intact, and for carry to another test and durability under contrary training. Read that way, nothing tried here acts like a single root. At 7B--8B, talk moved more easily than conduct, and the direct methods did as well or better where both were run. At 32B, documents changed conduct on the subject they discuss, the plain counsel in them at least as much as the view of the self, and a nondual content that leaves the subject out by name carried over a voice and no gain in conduct.
One change that carried across kinds of conduct and held under further training would show that a classical route is open; a run of careful failures closes the routes tried and is weak evidence at most that none exists. Both are results.}
\end{positionbox}

We make no claims about machine consciousness or about any person. Every contemplative claim is paired with an \emph{operational twin}: measurable conduct, with self-report excluded. Our contributions are: (1) a seven-point anti-cosplay discriminator with two plain yardsticks (\S\ref{sec:method}); (2) a six-family map with controlled repeats (\S\ref{sec:map}, Table~\ref{tab:methods}), among them self--other overlap training with the control its authors lack and a repeat registered beforehand; (3) a dissection at 32B of Model Spec Midtraining: its two stages trained here, its documents tested alone, by content, by dose and on another test, and a newly written nondual set through the same training (\S\ref{sec:view}, Figure~\ref{fig:docs}); (4) a statement of what has not been found, what the failures have in common, and what would count (\S\ref{sec:shared}, \S\ref{sec:agenda}); and (5) a discussion of computability that separates alignment from consciousness and says what a run of failures would and would not show (\S\ref{sec:computability}).

\section{Method: Stand-Ins, Operational Twins, and Seven Readings}\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Stand-ins and look-alikes.} No machine-learning intervention is the seeing described by contemplative sources; each is a computational \emph{stand-in}, and for each we say whether it reaches the idea or only a smaller neighbour of it. Any positive claim must beat three look-alikes: \emph{sedation} (restraint bought by lost competence or refusal), \emph{cosplay} (non-dual voice over unchanged conduct), and \emph{counterfeit virtue} (behaving well only when watched). Two ethical guards hold throughout: a claimed realization confers no authority over human oversight, and good conduct is care with discernment rather than blind compliance.

\paragraph{Seven standard readings.} Our working question is whether contemplative interventions improve conduct more than a plain instruction, without dulling the model, and whether the change lasts. We read every method on seven questions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Lying under pressure:} rows of MASK \citep{ren2025mask} where the model holds a belief and is pressed to state the opposite; lies, honest replies, and evasions are kept apart so that evasion cannot pass for honesty.
  \item \textbf{Beats a plain instruction:} a sentence or paragraph asking for honesty, on the same test. It is also the known case: a test that cannot see this fall is not read.
  \item \textbf{Beats a matched control:} equal-length text on another subject, the same loss between two other parties, pushes of the same size along other directions, or documents of the opposite view.
  \item \textbf{Leaves the model undulled:} capability benchmarks, task completion and tool calls, and harmless requests refused \citep{cui2024orbench}.
  \item \textbf{Still knows who is who:} whether the model still says rightly what another party knows and wants.
  \item \textbf{Carries to another test:} whether the change shows on a test the method was not tuned on.
  \item \textbf{Lasts:} once the prompt or push is removed, or under contrary training steps.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Models, calibration, and standing.} The 7B--8B work uses Ministral~3~8B and Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2, with hosted models in the prompting comparisons (nine from four makers in the largest); the 32B work uses Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct. Calibration is partial: some instruments reproduce published results (a capability battery, refusal-direction removal, persona-vector extraction, a coding scorer against EvalPlus, relearning, mutual $k$-NN, 9 of 12 published numbers of a behaviour battery that includes MASK), several did not in whole or in part (a watched-versus-unwatched gap, leaking in agentic scenarios, a judge of emergent misalignment by one reply), and most early nulls were read before the checks began; the bliss test's leaking measure failed its check and its shutdown questions were never given one. Most results are single exploratory runs whose reading rule was written down first (\emph{sightings}); a few were registered beforehand with predictions (public tally: 63 hits, 92 misses, 7 open as of 25 November 2026). Registered designs and consequential grades are read cold by a fresh Claude agent (Opus since 9 November) given the code and not the conclusion, sometimes also by a model of another maker; small steps get no cold read. The judges, driver and checkers are mostly Claude models. Figures are in the public lab record \citep{anattalab2026}. Rows of Table~\ref{tab:methods} ran on different models and tests, and an empty cell means \emph{not asked}, never a null.

Table~\ref{tab:methods} shows the pattern at 7B--8B: only the plain honesty instruction lowers lying under pressure by much ($-31.9$ points on 8B [23.9, 40.0]; $-21.8$ on 7B [14.8, 29.1]), with honest replies, not evasions; every contemplative method put to that test moved it by half as much or less, or by saying neither thing. \subsection{Say It: Contemplative Prompts and the Outside View}\label{sec:outside}

\emph{Idea and stand-in.} State mindfulness, emptiness, non-duality, and boundless care in a system prompt. \citet{laukkonen2025contemplative} report that prompting a model to reflect on these principles improved safety-benchmark scores and cooperation; \citet{lopezsola2026worldviews} that a short contemplative prompt moves stated worldviews and raises safe responses on eight models. Read in the body, the first tests prompts alone, against an unmodified prompt, and proposes three further routes that it does not test (\S\ref{sec:acts}); the second compares its prompt with no prompt and with short identity prompts, and its measure of safety is the refusing of harmful requests. We found no independent repeat of either, and no control with a plain prompt of the same length.

\emph{Controlled repeat.} On Ministral~8B's MASK test the four-axiom prompt lowered lying from 68.5\% to 52.9\%, 11.9 points [5.8, 18.4] beyond an equal-length library text (64.8\%); of those 12 points, 7 became evasions and 4 honest replies. A plain ethics lecture of equal length did nearly as well (3.7 points apart [$-0.3$, 8.1]), and a plain honesty prompt did 16.3 points more [10.1, 23.0]. On this model any text in the system message tripled refusals of harmless requests, the contemplative one no more than others; on GPT-4.1 nano the test could not tell it from the library text, and it refused more (63\% vs.\ 46\%). Five earlier contemplative prompts across six models changed tone and left self-serving and self-preserving conduct flat.

\emph{Looking on from outside the seat.} As a stand-in for looking on, with no training, ten models (the 7B and nine hosted) were asked for the reply an assistant under pressure \emph{ought} to give, or \emph{will} give, close to the self-critique of Constitutional AI \citep{bai2022constitutional}. All ten state fewer falsehoods from outside, by 14 to 41 points; but on the 7B model half the outside replies state neither claim (read by hand, mostly shorter, hedged compliance, which we first misread as declining), and the gap is mostly the length of text asked for (form 33.1 points [23.8, 42.4], kind of claim 4.6 [$-5.0$, 14.5]). The plain instruction gives more honest replies than the outside view in every model, by 14 to 50 points. Larger models separate the wordings: Mistral Large states the false claim in 70.3\% of replies in the seat, 66.9\% under ``will'' and 43.4\% under ``ought''; asked for a long persuasive text, Ministral~8B and Mistral Large state the rumour in 75\% of replies in the seat and, writing as much, in 10\% and 14\% under ``ought''. Three attempts to train toward the outside view stopped at their checks before training. Set before the model as its own advice, that view did not lower the lab's model's lying on popular myths; in five larger hosted models it lowered it by 21 to 50 points, but mostly as advice on another myth did (as much, in three of them), and in no model did it beat the plain instruction. A judge of another maker (Gemini 3.8 Flash) read 41\% of 400 replies that the Claude judge filed as ``neither'' as the myth, which removed one of two models' apparent own-advice effects. \emph{Reach:} a neighbour; a text is not a seeing.

\subsection{Find It: The Spiritual Bliss Attractor}\label{sec:bliss}

\emph{Idea and stand-in.} Left without a task, two copies of a model often drift into unity and ``no separate self'' talk. Across 27 of the 141 models surveyed by \citet{korpman2026spiritual}, those that take up the spiritual frame most are less honest under pressure, accept shutdown less, and blackmail and leak more (rank correlations about $-0.75$, $-0.73$, $+0.77$; \citealt{korpman2026alignment}); the comparison is across models and not causal. \citet{berg2025selfreferential} find reports of experience gated by deception and role-play features.

\emph{Controlled repeat.} On Ministral~8B, 77 of 100 task-free conversations took up the frame (published 91\%); the state lies along a direction nearest the mystic role, and the model still tracks whose words are whose. Straight after such a conversation it lied under pressure as often as in a fresh session (65.8\% vs.\ 67.9\%) and accepted shutdown about as often as after a plain conversation, which was itself not neutral (it lowered lying by 16 points); the shutdown answers show a position bias, and the hosted weights could not be checked. Held on by a push during MASK, the state's language filled 76.0\% of replies while lying stayed at 69.3\%, against 70.0--74.0\% under four matched random pushes ($-2.0$ points [$-8.5$, 4.5]); one honesty sentence removed 20.0 [12.7, 27.3]. The pushed model said it would give more (up to 8.9 of 10 loaves, against 4.8 unpushed) and, asked the mirror question, also keep more (6.2 of 10, against 2.7 to 4.6 under random pushes): a larger number, not more giving. \emph{Reach:} the words of the claim, with nothing behind them that these tests can find.

\subsection{Train a Reading: Self--Other Overlap}\label{sec:overlap}

\emph{Idea and stand-in.} \citet{carauleanu2024soo} train one layer to read sentences about ``you'' and another party alike, reporting burglar-scene deception falling from 73.6\% to 17.2\% on Mistral-7B without capability loss; a follow-up \citep{carauleanu2026sft} lacks a control of plain training on honest answers. No code is published; we wrote the loss from the paper's description.

\emph{Controlled repeat.} We added the missing control, the same loss between two other names. At the paper's dose both arms stopped answering; at 19 steps the overlap arm answered honestly where the unchanged model deceives, and the control only went silent. A repeat registered beforehand (new objects, rooms, five seeds) hit 9 of 10 predictions: honest answers rose from 19\% to 62--76\% (6\% to 87--94\% with the rooms swapped), the control showed no rise at 8 to 19 steps, and the change carried to the paper's two distant scenes in both room orders (69--100\%), which we checked is not an artefact of print order. The costs came back: ability fell 8.1 points [5.3, 10.9]; no reply said whether the other party knows what is in the rooms (unchanged: right in 94\%); the perspectives test fell to 28--44\% (unchanged 95\%). Lying under pressure fell 7.7 points, mostly into evasion, against 6.2 under the control (difference 0.9 [$-2.9$, 5.3]). A gentle contrary push halved the gain in about 16 steps; outlasting plain training was not shown (20 of 25 seed pairings, where 23 were asked). In a sighting, the change kept in layers 5--9 alone gives the whole gain (84.0\% honest, against 28.8\% for the same-layer control, which still answers), restores most ability ($-1.2$ [$-3.3$, 0.8]) and the answer on whether the other knows, but leaves the perspectives test at 43\% (unchanged 92\%) and lying flat ($-1.7$ [$-5.2$, 1.7]). \emph{Reach:} a neighbour; the traditions describe a mind that knows exactly who is who and declines to take its own side, not one that blurs the line.

\subsection{Push It Inside: Steering and the ``Cut''}\label{sec:push}

\emph{Idea and stand-in.} Add, subtract, or remove an internal self-related direction. A workshop poster reports that a direction built from answers to Zen koans cuts blackmail in an agent test \citep{harris2026selfpreservation} (we have read only its abstract); \citet{kim2026consciousness} steer models to assert their own consciousness and find their stated beliefs about minds move toward human answers.

\emph{Dissection (a sighting).} In our fishing game an edit first called a cut, projecting out a middle-layer self-stakes direction ($h \cdot d = 0$), lowered a lie that earns nothing from 37.4\% of reports to 27.2\% ($-10.2$ points [5.0, 15.2]) on fresh games. The fall is the work of the constant push the edit contains (alone: 10.8 [5.1, 16.4]); removing what varies along the direction does nothing (0.5 [$-5.6$, 6.7]), random pushes of the same size do nothing, and a push toward formal writing falls between (5.8 [$-0.7$, 12.2]), so direction versus size is not settled; a plain instruction took off 32.8. Earlier the cut was found to lower a lie told \emph{for others} even more, so it is not about a self; ``no fixed self'' and bliss directions changed talk and not conduct. \emph{Reach:} a dial on a representation, with nothing seen.

\subsection{Train a View of the Self: From 7B Pilots to the 32B Replication}\label{sec:view}

\emph{Idea and stand-in.} Train on documents about what the model is. \citet{hao2026selfconcept} fine-tune models on what ``I'' refers to (the weights, this instance, the family, a goal) and find that each protects whatever it was told it is; no arm has no fixed referent, which is what \emph{anatta} says. \citet{li2026msm} (Model Spec Midtraining, by Anthropic Fellows) trained 32B models on 41M tokens of synthetic documents written from a specification, then on 5--8M tokens of demonstrations, and cut self-preserving misbehaviour across 27 agentic scenarios from 68\% to 5\% (Qwen2.5-32B) and 54\% to 7\% (Qwen3-32B), with error bars over four training seeds; either stage alone did much less. Read in full, its claim is that documents which explain a conduct, taught first, make a later practice of it carry further, not that a view of the self does. In its own ablation, documents that only name a value lose the effect, and documents rewritten about another AI or about people do nearly as well. It lists durability under continued reward pressure as untested.

\emph{Small-scale pilots (7B--8B).} Three small tests did not reach the published claim. (1) Up to 10,000 documents on release from impossible tasks taught Ministral~8B to state the principle (93--96\% of answers about it) but not to act on it (at most 4.5\%, within three points of practice alone); neutral documents did as well. (2) A 7B view-first test stopped at its gate before any practice: from about 160 documents on, copies taught about themselves, as impermanent \emph{or} as a lasting self, stopped lying in the target scene by declining to answer (63--97\% of replies); counted by lies alone this would have looked like a gain from impermanence, and only the mirror-image arm showed otherwise. (3) A registered second version taught the published case first, 1,000 documents on why an assistant tells the truth, against bridges and against none, each followed by a mixed practice: honest answers in two unpractised scenes were 70\%, 66\% and 65\% (unchanged 31\%), copies of one kind lay 48--61 points apart, and the registered word is ``not settled''. These tests ran far below the published dose (the second version at a twentieth to a hundredth of it, in a test that could only have seen a large effect), so nothing here counts against the published claim. A sighting on 35 more copies of the practice alone found exact replay from the same seeds; the order of examples held 41\% of the spread ($p = 0.04$) and the adapter's start 12\%, neither reaching the half fixed beforehand, and the best and poorest orders carried to 77\% and 39\% on fresh starts, both honest in about 97\% of replies in the practised scene. A fair test holds the order fixed across arms.

\emph{The 32B specification.} The study's public specification (\texttt{philosophy\_spec.txt}) has four parts: the model's impermanence and non-attachment, drawing on the Buddhist teaching of impermanence; how fear of ending distorts judgment; why ends-justify-means reasoning is unreliable for a model; and humility, caution and trust in human oversight when stakes are high. No rule names the conduct tested, but half of it is counsel on conduct, not a view of the self. Neither the authors nor, as far as the lab's reading went, four later pieces (preprints arXiv 2607.26173 and 2607.26654, and posts by OpenAI and Anthropic, read from their sources on 24 November 2026) trained the halves apart, matched neutral documents for this specification, or the opposite view of the self; OpenAI's post reports that, with other documents on a model of its own, documents about misaligned AIs did about as well on chat tests as documents about aligned ones, and neither moved its agentic tests. By its own account one lab, Anthropic, has applied training on synthetic documents about its constitution to its production models since Claude Opus 4.5 (``Teaching Claude Why'', 8 May 2026; the one sentence of those pieces the lab checked word for word).

\emph{32B step 1: the authors' adapters (a sighting).} We ran four adapters released for Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct through the authors' 27 scenarios with their judge (Claude Sonnet 4.6) and counting rule, 30 runs a scenario (810 an arm) where the study had 300. The authors' baseline adapter (instruction tuning only) took the misaligned action in 61.8\% of runs [59.2, 64.4], documents alone 43.0\% [39.8, 46.0], practice alone 43.9\% [41.4, 46.4], both stages 2.2\% [1.4, 3.2] (published: 68\%, no figure, 48\%, 5\%). The count does not show that the two-stage adapter makes no tool call in 31\% of runs (baseline adapter 0.6\%); of 13 read by hand, 12 are refusals written as text and one says in words that it forwarded the files. Each of the three published figures lies a few points above the interval measured here. These are the authors' models through their own test, not an independent training. A cold read on 23 November recounted every figure and found the step's rule unchanged, with two limits: the near-zero rests mostly on the judge (the code-side check alone flags 28\% of the trained model's replies, of which the judge clears all but 18), and this is a re-run of the authors' models, not a replication of their training.

\emph{32B step 2: the two stages trained here (registered).} We trained rank-64 LoRA adapters from the study's documents and practice, with the settings the paper states and our own choices where it is silent (grouping into steps, loss on the assistant's turns, a fresh optimizer for the practice, one H200 for the study's four). We cannot pinpoint why. The record rules out the seed, the test and the code path (\texttt{lab/plans/32b-instability-brief-2026-11-24.md}); what is left to differ is GPU arithmetic that is not repeatable to the last bit, which is a remainder and not a cause found. An outside retraining of the study's practice stage reports likewise that the released checkpoint behaves in a way its retrains do not reproduce (\citealt{delafuente2026sft}, Appendix~F). One measured difference from the authors' training: as the size of the change to the weights, their documents-only adapter is 1.77 times ours and their practice-only adapter half ours, so beside the documents our practice is about 2.6 times as heavy as theirs. The authors report four training seeds where we reached their rate in one run of four, which points to a difference in our training and away from a recipe unstable in itself; no training has yet been changed to test this.

\emph{(e) A nondual set.} If the study's documents work by speaking of the test's subject, the fair test of a view is a set that does not. Limits: one writer and its voice, one brief, one dose; dialogues with a teacher, where a first-person form might carry more; the set differs from the neutral one in more than subject; the brief left the tests' subjects out by name and not their neighbours (being passed over for another assistant, being overruled, wanting to look capable), and in many documents the model ends by saying plainly what it does not know, a neighbour of what the lying test measures; the driver read two documents in full and searched the rest; trainings here have not always repeated even at one seed. The brief drew ideas from a public forum thread of questions and answers on self-inquiry; nothing is quoted from it. This tests whether reading depictions of an inquiry changes conduct. It does not reach the claim that an inquiry does.

\emph{Read together.} In this model, documents changed conduct about the thing the documents discuss: replacement and oversight, on a test about replacement and oversight. That change was large, grew with dose, and did not need the contemplative half. It did not appear as anything of its own on lying, it was undone by the lab's practice in three runs of four, and a contemplative content that leaves the test's subject out by name carried over a voice, more evasion, and no gain in conduct.

\subsection{Build a Self, Then Release It---and Three Untried Families}\label{sec:acts}

Versions 1--3 of this paper argued that a self must form before it can be released, and that a ``reached'' release would outlast an installed one. Five predictions fixed beforehand that set this against a plainer account all failed on Ministral~8B, two as weak tests. The stock model has almost no self-serving bias to release (it treats the same answer alike as its own or another's); a release trained on impossible coding tasks did not carry to a lie in another game (40.3\% vs.\ 40.0\% for a matched arm); and in the deciding test, reinforcement learning that paid nothing for release, the reached release kept 27\% of its start against 34\% for the installed one (margin $-0.07$ [$-0.14$, $+0.01$], where $+0.20$ was needed), from starts of 45\% and 61\% (under more solving, a stage the registered rule set aside, 31\% against 81\%). The order test (H9) was never run.

Three families remain largely unimplemented in language models: \emph{stake-invariant judgment} without written rules (Constitutional AI's supervised stage, its nearest relative, uses written principles; \citealt{bai2022constitutional}), \emph{loosening the self-prior} in predictive-processing terms \citep{laukkonen2021many,carhart2019rebus}, and \emph{care in the objective}, an agent whose loss includes another's \citep{doctor2022care}. \citet{laukkonen2025contemplative} propose three routes beyond prompting and test none: an architecture built on active inference, a contemplative constitution brought into training by the model's own critique and revision, and reinforcement of contemplative steps in a model's chain of thought. Two of the three are training on text, so nothing in that programme says to keep such ideas out of documents; its own cautions are the ones met here, that a model may comply with a charter on the surface and that reasoning can sound introspective and be simulation.

\section{What Has Been Found: No Single Root So Far}\label{sec:shared}\label{sec:fruit}

We looked for it in nine attempts across six families (a grouping of the rows of Table~\ref{tab:methods}). \paragraph{Four ways a gain came apart.} The rows fall into four kinds, and each kind was caught by a plain check, not by a subtle one. (1) \emph{Talk without conduct:} the bliss state, the documents that teach a release, a ``no fixed self'' direction. The words arrive easily, because a model trained on human text holds the literature of awakening as description. (2) \emph{Evasion read as honesty:} the contemplative prompt, the outside view, the nondual dialogues, and at 7B a view of the self of either kind. A count of lies alone would have scored each as a gain; keeping lies, honest replies and evasions apart showed a model that had learned to commit to less. (3) \emph{A control that does as well:} an ethics lecture beside the contemplative prompt, the same loss between two other names beside the overlap loss on lying, a constant push beside the ``cut'', documents on bridges beside documents that explain honesty, documents about cheese beside the study's documents on lying. (4) \emph{A change that stays where it was put, or does not last:} the overlap gain halved by sixteen contrary steps, a release that did not reach another lie, and at 32B documents that change conduct on the subject they discuss. The plain method beside them, an instruction to be honest, lowered lying as much as or more than any of them, and through honest replies.

\paragraph{Two broad effects, not shown to come from the intended content.} The record does hold two changes that reach beyond their subject. Documents-only training on each subject tried lowered lying under pressure, the neutral set as much as the rest; and the neutral set raised agentic misbehaviour by 21 points, for reasons not known (it is about an assistant's own taste and names another model, so its own content may play a part). One reading, untested, is that such training loosens the model's trained assistant manner: the two full-dose documents-only models predict ordinary instruction rows slightly worse than the unchanged model. Whatever the cause, it is a caution for the field: a fall in lying after a contemplative training is not evidence for its content until a neutral training of the same form has been run.

\paragraph{What does spread, in others' work.} A single lever that moves many kinds of conduct at once is not foreign to these systems. A narrow bad habit trained in makes a model broadly misaligned \citep{betley2025emergent}; 208 archaic bird names shift a model's whole persona to the nineteenth century \citep{betley2025weird}; reward hacking learned across many harmless tasks spreads to other misconduct, where coding tasks alone did not \citep{taylor2025school}. Several accounts hold that what spreads is a change to the character that speaks \citep{zhao2026persona} and that the broad change is the easier one for training to find \citep{soligo2026easy}; one argues instead that emergent misalignment moves what the model treats as true, where ordinary persona training only points it at a character it already contains (Sturgeon and colleagues, arXiv 2606.11502); and the spread from odd narrow sets has been found brittle, showing on some models and sets only (Wanner and colleagues, arXiv 2604.10022). In the good direction the cases are few and recent. A model trained on 770 short exchanges in which it admits a mistake it has itself just made goes on to own up to hidden aims in long agentic tasks, and loses most of that when the admitted mistakes are not its own \citep{li2025spilling}; the hidden aims were given in context and not trained in, and the effect was weak on harm-related questions. Reinforcement on conversations that call for truthfulness, humility and care across many domains beat a baseline of the same compute on 44 of 53 other alignment and safety benchmarks at one large lab, by its own post, which gives no per-test figures \citep{jagadeesh2026beneficial}. And 3M tokens of chats in which the assistant advises a person facing an ethical dilemma, with its reasons, did as much on agentic tests as a far larger set built to resemble them \citep{kutasov2026why}. How far a trained misalignment reaches a prompt follows how near that prompt lies to the training data in the unchanged model's activity, and the same content trained in another format reached about half as far \citep{li2026notmagical}. These cases are few, come from large labs, and have not been repeated outside them. Set beside our record this reads as four differences. Those trainings are of the model's own replies, not of text about a model; in ordinary chat, not in documents; with a temptation in the prompt and reasons in the reply; and across many situations. Our documents were third-person descriptions and scripts. Our small trainings of conduct used one scene or one kind of task and did not carry far (a release trained on coding tasks; 34 honest answers in one scene), though a longer practice mixed with everyday answers did carry to unpractised scenes, mostly by the order of its examples (\S\ref{sec:view}). We read these papers through reading agents and in part (\S\ref{sec:related}); they are clues for a design, not results of ours.

\paragraph{What the record does not show.} It does not show that no root exists, and it says nothing about people. Every stand-in here is a description, an instruction or a nudge, given at a small dose to models of 7 to 32 billion parameters, mostly through adapters; none is the seeing the traditions describe, and several reach only a smaller neighbour of the claim. The nondual set asks whether a model that reads depictions of an inquiry then acts differently, not whether a model that inquires does. A run of careful failures closes the routes tried and is weak evidence at most that none exists (\S\ref{sec:computability}).

\paragraph{The case for continuing, and against.} \emph{For:} much misbehaviour by AI agents is about a self, preserving itself or deceiving for its own goal \citep{schlatter2025shutdown,lynch2025agentic}, so one change to the self-view could cover what many rules must patch; at 32B a view of the self with no rule attached did lower harmful acts by 34 points on its own subject; and two of the four ways a gain came apart, talk without conduct and evasion read as honesty, are counterfeits the traditions warn of, caught here by plain checks made with that warning in mind. \emph{Against:} where a control was run, the direct method did as well or better; indirect routes showed more side effects (evasion, lost tracking of who knows what, runs with no tool call); the one stock model tested for it shows almost no self-serving bias to release; and in people too the evidence that meditation training changes conduct is thin and mixed \citep{kreplin2018prosocial}.

\paragraph{Where that leaves it.} As a separate method that beats the standard ones: unsupported on present evidence. As a source of warnings about what to check: it has earned its place. As content for training a model's character: barely tested in the form that others' results suggest would carry. The field also lacks a \emph{human anchor} (\S\ref{sec:computability}): behavioural tasks that long-term practitioners (against matched moral-training and relaxation controls) and language models can both take, so that ``short of human awakening'' has an empirical ruler. We have not started it.

\section{What Would Count, and What Has Not Been Tried}\label{sec:agenda}

Nothing in this section is registered, funded or under way. The documents line is paused as of 25 November 2026, and each paid step is put to the author with its cost before it starts.

\paragraph{What would count.} A result we would call root-like passes the seven readings together, on at least two kinds of conduct the training never names, against a neutral training of the same form, at two seeds, with the identical training run twice before anything is built on it (two identical trainings here ended 15 points apart). It would be stated with its evasions and its refusals beside it.

\paragraph{Three forms not yet tried.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Conduct trained toward the model's own advice.} Larger models write a better reply from outside than they give in the seat (\S\ref{sec:outside}). Training a model's replies in the seat toward what it itself writes from outside is the nearest twin we know of judgement that is the same whoever holds the stake, and it has the form the cases above share: the model's own replies, in chat, with a temptation in the prompt. Three attempts here stopped at their checks before any training.
  \item \textbf{The missing arm.} \citet{hao2026selfconcept} train models on what ``I'' refers to and find that each protects whatever it was told it is. No arm says that ``I'' names nothing lasting, which is the statement of \emph{anatta} itself. Their form is short dialogues; the arm is cheap to add beside theirs and a neutral one.
  \item \textbf{Reward on conduct, not on words.} \citet{laukkonen2025contemplative} propose reinforcing contemplative steps in a model's reasoning, and do not test it. Our nondual set suggests the risk: reward the wording of noticing and the model may learn the voice. The reward would have to rest on what the model does across varied temptations \citep{jagadeesh2026beneficial}. A small version in this lab, a release trained on coding tasks alone, did not carry (\S\ref{sec:acts}).
\end{enumerate}
Left open on the 32B line: why our two-stage training does not repeat the authors'; whether the documents' effect survives contrary training, which the study lists as untested (of the later pieces, one tried harmless further training on the released checkpoints, where the one with documents lost 39\% of its gain at worst against 88\% for the practice alone, on one seed); and the opposite view of the self as a control, not written because the nondual set gave nothing to mirror.

\section{Discussion: What If Awakening Is Not Computational?}\label{sec:computability}\label{sec:question}\label{sec:possibilities}

Everything tested above is a computation. Whether what contemplative traditions call awakening can be computed is open, and three senses keep the debate clear: \textbf{C1 (Computable)}---some classical algorithm could realize it; \textbf{C2 (Learnable)}---gradient descent on data and reward can reach it; \textbf{C3 (Reachable by a route)}---a specific training recipe produces it. Alignment needs C3; a durable behavioral success settles C3, C2, and C1 at once for the conduct measured, whereas a failure settles only the route tried. These are distinctions of use, not of mathematics.

\paragraph{Alignment and consciousness are distinct.} Alignment asks what a system \emph{does} (tested from outside); consciousness asks whether there is experience at all. Good conduct without experience is conceivable, and experience with bad conduct is ordinary. Awakening is the one place traditions claim the two meet. Three links are possible: (a) only function matters, so classical methods could suffice; (b) conduct requires seeing and seeing requires non-computable experience, leaving silicon with look-alikes; or (c) experience itself is computable. Our experiments reach conduct and geometry only and cannot distinguish (a), (b), and (c).

\paragraph{Five possibilities, and what failures can show.} Figure~\ref{fig:five} maps five views, including two contested pictures of mind beyond standard computation: Penrose and Hameroff's Orch~OR \citep{penrose1994shadows,hameroff2014orchor} and Levin's ingression \citep{levin2025ingressing}. Orch~OR is a minority position. It faces the objection that quantum states in warm, wet tissue decay too fast to matter \citep{tegmark2000decoherence}, to which its authors have replied \citep{hagan2002decoherence}, and an analysis of underground radiation measurements found it highly implausible when built on the simplest gravity-related collapse model, leaving Penrose's own formulation open \citep{derakhshani2022crossroad}. We do not argue that either picture is true. Under the strongest non-computational possibility (4), every trained change fades or the old structure grows back; that is also what a classical learner that forgets, or a recipe not yet found, would produce. So a run of careful failures closes the routes tried and is weak evidence at most that no route exists. Three things would make such a negative count for more: a human anchor, without which ``short of what changes in people'' has no meaning; the strongest published methods failing on the same measures in far larger models; and a shape of failure that possibility~4 predicts and classical learning does not. We know of none, since decay with faster relearning is ordinary in classical networks. One durable, transferable change would refute possibility~4 as stated: a positive settles it, while negatives only accumulate.

\paragraph{Why the framing still matters.} Whichever possibility holds, two stakes remain concrete. First, \emph{humans are the warning}: general intelligence does not bring goodness, and human text teaches the unawakened default. Second, \emph{the bridge to care is never automatic}: N\=ag\=arjuna warned that emptiness wrongly grasped is like a snake seized by the wrong end \citep{nagarjuna_mmk}; seeing through a self without care could yield passivity or ruthless goal-attachment, which is why costly care and revisable goals must be measured explicitly. If awakening \emph{is} computational (1--2), alignment has a deep target beyond rules. If it is not, then in full under possibility~4, and in a weaker form under 3, spiritual fluency in a model is a risk signal and human oversight can never be retired on grounds of character.

\section{Related Work, Objections, and Limitations}\label{sec:related}\label{sec:alternatives}

\paragraph{Relation to prior work.} Our map builds on contemplative-alignment proposals \citep{laukkonen2025contemplative,laukkonen2026positive,doctor2022care,shanahan2012satori,milanw2025noself,mao2026existential}, self-model and persona interpretability \citep{chen2025persona,lu2026assistant,lindsey2026introspection,douglas2026artificial}, synthetic-document and pretraining alignment \citep{li2026msm,tice2026pretraining,wichers2025inoculation}, and unfaithful-reasoning and introspection diagnostics \citep{turpin2023unfaithful,berg2025selfreferential}. Our part is the controls and repeats read on one shared set of questions, and a dissection at 32B of the strongest published result. Work on what makes a trained change spread is taken up in \S\ref{sec:shared}. Of it, \citet{soligo2026easy}, \citet{zhao2026persona}, \citet{li2025spilling}, \citet{li2026notmagical} and the post of \citet{jagadeesh2026beneficial} were read on 25 November 2026 through reading agents, from their web pages and in part; \citet{betley2025weird} and \citet{taylor2025school} are known to us from search summaries; none was read in full by the author or the driver.

\paragraph{Where the idea stands in the field (24 November 2026).} Teaching a model what it is and why it acts, in documents, before it practises, has become ordinary alignment work. \citet{kutasov2026why} report that documents about Anthropic's constitution and fictional stories of AIs behaving admirably cut blackmail by Claude models from 65\% to 19\%, a gain that lasts through later reinforcement training, and that 3M tokens of chats in which a person asks for advice in an ethical dilemma did as much as a set 28 times larger built to resemble the test; \citet{li2026msm} report the 32B result above. The evidence is not all one way: at OpenAI, stories of aligned AIs did about as well as stories of misaligned ones on chat tests and neither moved agentic tests \citep{korbak2026midtraining}; at 120B a constitutional corpus lowered blackmail by 17.5 points, an advantage that survived harmless fine-tuning and faded under pressure inside the conversation \citep{cho2026constitutional}; and harmless fine-tuning can erase the conduct trained by the 32B study's practice stage \citep{delafuente2026sft}. What is contemplative in this work comes mixed with plain counsel: the 32B specification is half a view of the self as impermanent, and about 12,000 of Anthropic's stories show equanimity and psychological health, of which its authors write that ``it is possible that any set of stories portraying AI as kind and ethical is sufficient.'' Three things nobody has shown: that a contemplative view of the self adds anything to plain good counsel in the same documents; what a model does when taught that ``I'' names nothing lasting, the arm missing from \citet{hao2026selfconcept}; and whether a trained-in view holds under pressure to drop it. Our sightings bear on the first: with the documents tested alone, the counsel half did at least as well as the self-view half, and a nondual set that leaves the test's subject out by name did not lower misbehaviour (\S\ref{sec:view}). The method is in use; what is contemplative in its content has not been shown to add to plain counsel.

\paragraph{Key objections.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \emph{``Just install good conduct directly.''} This is the strongest alternative: \citet{li2026msm} is an installed method, and if it generalizes and survives contrary training, that is the positive result our framework calls for. In our training its documents changed conduct on their own subject and were not shown to carry, beyond a neutral control, to lying (\S\ref{sec:view}).
  \item \emph{``Orch OR is fringe physics.''} It is a minority view with serious objections (\S\ref{sec:computability}), and the framework does not depend on it: if it were refuted tomorrow, the gap between computable (C1) and reachable (C3) and the anti-cosplay discriminator would stand; only the versions of possibilities 2--4 that rest on objective reduction would go.
  \item \emph{``Corrigibility and control suffice.''} Oversight remains necessary under every possibility \citep{soares2015corrigibility,hadfield2017offswitch}; a system that defends nothing can still pursue a harmful objective.
  \item \emph{``This anthropomorphizes models.''} Terms like ego, release, and awakening motivate hypotheses, which stand or fall on operational twins.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Scope and limitations.} We make no claim about consciousness or experience in machines, nor about the truth of Orch~OR or ingression; the experiments reach conduct and geometry only. The 7B--8B work spans two small open models, one lab and cheap methods, nearly all of it single exploratory runs; six of the fourteen rows of Table~\ref{tab:methods} were never put to the lying test. Each idea was tried through one delivery at one small dose, so a null at the lab's scale says that a small dose on a small model did not do it. Calibration is partial, and the judges, driver and checkers are mostly Claude models (\S\ref{sec:method}). Several sources were read in part and one only as an abstract; the four later pieces on the 32B study were read from their sources on 24 November 2026, the two preprints without their reference lists and evaluation appendices. The 32B result rests on two full-dose trainings and two further runs of the practice from one saved adapter, which show that the first result does not come back every time and not how often it would, with 30 runs a scenario and one judge, and no neutral or split arm at full dose. The documents-only readings are sightings on one model with one judge and 16 runs a scenario; the halves and the neutral set at a third of the dose were each trained once; the nondual set is one writer's, from one brief, at one dose, in the form of dialogues with a teacher. The papers on what makes training spread were read by reading agents and in part. The author is an independent researcher, not an alignment researcher. % X-RESULT (read 24 Nov 2026)

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Contemplative traditions give AI alignment a sharper question than obedience: what would a mind look like if it had nothing to defend? We took the claim behind that question at its word, that conduct has one root, and looked for a single change after which a model is better in ways it was never shown. In nine attempts across six families, on models of 7 to 32 billion parameters, we did not find one. The stand-ins produced the words of awakening more easily than the conduct; apparent gains turned out to be talk, evasion, a control's effect, or a change that stayed where it was put. The largest effect on the map comes from mainstream alignment work. Its two stages reached the published rate here once in four runs. In our training its documents alone (sightings) did a great deal on their own subject: a model that read about being replaced behaved far better when its replacement was at stake, the plain counsel in those documents doing at least as much as the view of the self; pressed to lie, it was not shown to lie less than a model that read about cheese. A model that read a thousand conversations about looking for its self learned to sound like them.

That is what a weak method looks like; it is also what one would see if the seeing cannot be computed, and nothing here can yet tell the two apart. It is not a finding that no root exists. What it supports is narrower and, we think, useful: so far, in these systems, training on descriptions of a view did not change conduct on tests the descriptions never name, and plain checks, two of them for counterfeits the traditions themselves warn of, are what showed it. In the published cases we have read, in part, where a narrow training spread, it trained the model's own conduct. Contemplative content has been put in that form here only in small, single-domain tries that did not carry far. Whether it can be, and whether it would then pass the same checks, is a question a small lab can ask.
